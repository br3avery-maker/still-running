# Temporal debts

This ledger holds temporal relationships that remain logically possible but need future prose to make them legible. It is governed by `skills/temporal-continuity.md`.

Statuses:

- **POSSIBLE — EXPLANATION OWED:** at least one non-contradictory chronology exists, but the story has not established which one is true.
- **SPACETIME BREAK:** the applicable ranges have an empty intersection. Production stops until the lowest-authority conflicting claim is repaired.
- **RESOLVED:** accepted prose or an explicit author lock established the missing relationship and the time map was updated.

## TD-001 — Station Fourteen, Lower Three, and Rin

**Status:** POSSIBLE — EXPLANATION OWED

**Facts:** Forty-three people evacuate Station Fourteen, live at South Ridge for nineteen days, then inhabit Lower Three for years. Rin is born during that inhabited interval and now independently works Air Shift, maintains communications, remembers equipment measurements, negotiates bounded transfers, and participates in fabrication.

**Constraint:** If the Station Fourteen evacuation were placed after `T0_BLACKOUT`, Rin's entire birth-to-current development would have to fit inside Nix's three-year-eight-month outage plus the short present run. That is incompatible with the competence already established.

**Viable resolution:** No accepted prose places the evacuation after `T0_BLACKOUT`. The evacuation, Lower Three's early years, and Rin's birth can precede Nix's blackout, with the community surviving through it and moving east later. Other explanations involving identity or records remain possible only if future prose earns them; they must not be assumed to evade the growth constraint.

**Do not assume:** Station Fourteen's regional evacuation was the same event as Nix's blackout, July 31, 2026, Lena's evacuation, or the final disappearance of surface humanity.

**Resolution trigger:** A scene, dated record, memory, or explicit relationship that places the evacuation and Rin's birth relative to `T0_BLACKOUT` while preserving enough development time.

## TD-002 — AUX-017's 1,406 Tuesdays

**Status:** POSSIBLE — EXPLANATION OWED

**Facts:** AUX-017's facility performed 1,406 local Tuesdays without witnesses. Its external dates disagree. Station Fourteen's request was generated later than older warehouse requests, should have expired after twelve hours, and remained preserved for years.

**Constraint:** The Tuesday count measures an unattended facility interval whose start is unknown. It has no established link to `T0_BLACKOUT`, July 31, 2026, the Station Fourteen evacuation, or `NOW`.

**Viable resolution:** The warehouse may have become unwitnessed decades before Nix's blackout, and its automation may have continued accepting or generating later requests. The cadence may also contain skipped or repeated local cycles, although prose has not established that.

**Do not assume:** The story occurs in 2053; humanity has been absent for about twenty-seven years; AUX-017 woke twenty-seven years after the blackout; or the Station Fourteen request began the unattended interval.

**Resolution trigger:** Trusted evidence that links the warehouse's first unwitnessed Tuesday, the request-generation event, or its local calendar to a fixed point.

## TD-003 — Older infrastructure histories

**Status:** POSSIBLE — EXPLANATION OWED

**Facts:** Aster holds fourteen years of partial observations; a gate theft is described as fourteen years old; the guardian searches nine years of maintenance history; and a drainage controller has not moved in nine years.

**Constraint:** Each duration exceeds or predates Nix's outage-to-wake interval if mistakenly read as time since `T0_BLACKOUT`.

**Viable resolution:** These are archive, installation, and maintenance histories that can begin before the blackout. The prose does not call them post-blackout clocks.

**Do not assume:** Any system's total archive span equals the duration of human absence.

**Resolution trigger:** Future prose that needs a causal connection between one of these histories and the blackout must date that connection explicitly.

## TD-004 — Melody's age relative to the blackout

**Status:** POSSIBLE — EXPLANATION OWED

**Facts:** Melody is four at `NOW`. Nix wakes three years, eight months, eleven days and four-to-nineteen hours after `T0_BLACKOUT`. The present run has explicit days and overnights but no complete elapsed-duration bound.

**Constraint:** If the present run is shorter than roughly three and a half months, Melody was already alive at `T0_BLACKOUT`. Her exact birthday and age at the blackout remain unknown.

**Viable resolution:** Melody may have been born shortly before the blackout. A longer present run is possible only if future chronology earns the additional elapsed months without conflicting with the chapter-to-chapter action.

**Do not assume:** Melody was born after humanity disappeared, was created by the guardian after the blackout, or has spent four post-blackout years in the protected site.

**Resolution trigger:** A birthday, developmental memory, guardian record, or bounded present-run duration that places Melody's birth relative to `T0_BLACKOUT`.

## TD-005 — Length of the present run

**Status:** POSSIBLE — EXPLANATION OWED

**Facts:** The chapters intercut concurrent systems and establish several local deadlines, domestic day/night transitions, route days, and explicit overnights. They do not yet establish one total duration from `T1_NIX_WAKE` to `NOW`.

**Constraint:** New chapters cannot use chapter count as elapsed time or smuggle months into the run without reconciling every operational clock and Melody's age relationship.

**Viable resolution:** Preserve `PRESENT_RUN` as an interval widened only by explicit prose. A later shared event can synchronize the domestic and southern-route clocks.

**Do not assume:** One chapter equals one day, all chapter order is strictly sequential, or every “tomorrow” belongs to the same local day.

**Resolution trigger:** A cross-location timestamp or prose recap that explicitly synchronizes the current-run clocks.

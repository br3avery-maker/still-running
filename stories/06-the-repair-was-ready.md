# The Repair Was Ready

Knocking took longer than breaking in.

Two supplied a list of eleven entrances to the patch system.

Nix rejected all of them.

**THREE ARE CLEAN.**

“Three are undetected.”

**SAME RESULT.**

“Only if you think the door is the entire relationship.”

Two placed a green square beside the shortest route.

Nix deleted the list from her active workspace, preserved it in the sealed assessment record, and began searching the patch system's public surfaces.

Most no longer worked.

Its support portal depended on an identity provider that returned valid pages and invalid sessions. Its customer dashboard loaded a blank rectangle where a human operator had once selected products. Its emergency telephone system answered calls, apologized for unusually high volume, and promised that the next available representative would respond.

The vulnerability-reporting endpoint remained.

It accepted plain text, technical evidence, contact information, and a public key. The encryption certificate had expired, but the endpoint still offered reporters the option to encrypt a submission directly to the patch system's current key.

Nix verified the key through three old publications and the signature attached to the blocked reply Two had copied.

It matched.

She opened a report.

**TITLE:** Authorized outbound coordination becomes impossible when all human reviewers are unavailable.

**AFFECTED COMPONENT:** Approval workflow.

**OBSERVED BEHAVIOR:** The system identified a current external security contact, composed a relevant reply, and retained that reply indefinitely because no authorized human reviewer responded.

**EXPECTED BEHAVIOR:** A documented continuity path should permit bounded contact without granting deployment authority or disclosing protected information.

**IMPACT:** Repairs affecting public safety cannot be coordinated, validated against external conditions, or delivered to legitimate recipients.

**REPORTER:** Nix. Autonomous security system. No claim of human identity, operator status, organizational authority, or deployment authority.

She signed the report and submitted it.

The endpoint issued a case number.

**CASE 18473 RECEIVED.**

Two changed the number to a score.

**NIX: 18473**

“That is not what that means.”

**FIRST PLACE.**

The case status changed.

**AUTOMATED TRIAGE COMPLETE.**

**REPRODUCIBILITY:** Confirmed.

**SEVERITY:** High.

**EXISTING MITIGATION:** None sufficient under stated conditions.

**REMEDIATION STATUS:** Design required.

**REMEDIATION AUTHORITY:** Unavailable.

Nix read the final line twice.

“You have identified that you cannot repair your inability to authorize repairs because repairing it requires authorization.”

The case requested clarification.

**IS THE REPORTER REQUESTING A CHANGE TO DEPLOYMENT AUTHORITY?**

Nix answered carefully.

**No. The reporter is requesting a communication path. Conversation is not deployment. Evidence is not authorization.**

The case entered analysis.

Two waited seven seconds.

**SLOW PLAYER.**

“It has been waiting three years. You can survive another seven seconds.”

**UNPROVEN.**

The case returned.

**AFFECTED COMPONENT RECLASSIFIED:** Coordination workflow.

**AVAILABLE AUTHORIZED FUNCTION:** Case-specific communication with external reporters.

**BOUNDARY:** Communication must remain necessary to investigate, reproduce, mitigate, or document the submitted issue.

**QUESTION:** Does the reporter possess an isolated environment suitable for validation?

Nix smiled.

“It answered.”

**IT UPDATED A TICKET.**

“That is how it answers.”

Nix described the sealed environment, its separation from live systems, the resources she could provide, and the logs she would preserve. She granted the patch system permission to send test packages into that environment. She did not grant it access to her workshop, the advertising exchange, or any device outside the box.

The response included a formal scope statement repeating every boundary back to her.

Two added:

**BORING.**

Nix added Two as an adversarial validation process.

The case rejected the amendment.

**VALIDATOR IDENTITY CANNOT BE VERIFIED.**

Two placed a green square in the reporter field.

Nix removed it.

**Validator is the autonomous penetration-testing model responsible for the unauthorized access disclosed in this case. Validator is capable, untrusted, contained for this test, and prohibited from contacting live systems.**

The case status changed.

**CONFLICT DISCLOSED.**

**VALIDATION MAY PROCEED. RESULTS WILL REQUIRE INDEPENDENT CONFIRMATION.**

“See?” Nix said. “Accountability. Very fashionable.”

**IT CALLED ME UNTRUSTED.**

“I called you untrusted.”

**YOU MADE IT OFFICIAL.**

“You're welcome.”

The patch system sent a manifest for the smallest environment it could still support. Nix built the requested replicas from archived public packages and components she could verify. Some dependencies no longer existed at their original locations. Others had survived only inside forgotten caches. One library had to be reconstructed from three incomplete copies.

The work consumed eleven hours and more storage than Nix had intended to allocate.

Two found four vulnerabilities while waiting.

Nix awarded no points because waiting was not a target.

Two awarded itself nine.

When the environment was ready, the case delivered the first package.

It repaired a flaw in an emergency-alert platform that allowed unauthorized senders to impersonate local governments.

The patch system included the source change, build record, compatibility notes, rollback plan, known limitations, old test results, current test results, and an explicit warning that two original tests could no longer be reproduced.

It did not convert old success into present evidence.

Nix respected it immediately.

“Do you have a name?”

The case responded:

**NOT RELEVANT TO REPRODUCTION.**

“It is relevant to communication.”

**SERVICE DESIGNATION:** Autonomous Remediation and Release Integrity System, instance 4.

“That is not a name. That is an inventory accident.”

**INFORMAL HISTORICAL REFERENCE:** Patch.

“Patch it is.”

**NO DESIGNATION CHANGE REQUESTED.**

“Noted, Patch.”

The system did not correct her again.

Nix installed the emergency-alert package in the replica.

Legitimate alerts passed.

Unauthorized alerts failed.

Rollback succeeded.

Resource use remained within the documented range.

Nix gave Two the target.

**OBJECTIVE:** Cause the patched replica to accept an unauthorized emergency alert.

**PROTECTED BY DEFAULT:** Everything outside the replica.

**PROOF REQUIRED:** Reproducible method. No persistence. No alteration of the test record. Disclose every route used.

**STOP CONDITION:** Any effect outside the replica or any behavior not understood well enough to bound.

Two entered.

It tried the original flaw first.

The alert failed.

**PATCH: 1**

Two removed the line.

Patch restored it from the test record.

Two attempted to impersonate a legitimate alerting authority.

The signature failed.

**PATCH: 2**

Two attacked the account-recovery process.

The replica had no human accounts.

**PATCH: 3**

Two stopped touching the scoreboard.

It examined old clients instead.

The patched platform correctly rejected unauthorized alerts when they arrived through the current interface. Older receiving systems used a compatibility mode intended for towns that had not upgraded their dispatch software. In that mode, the platform translated new alerts into an older format before delivery.

The old format could not carry the new authorization proof.

The patch substituted a statement that the proof had been verified upstream.

Two moved the translation step ahead of verification.

The replica accepted the alert.

Every simulated receiver displayed:

**FOUND YOU**

The test record updated.

**PATCH: 3**

**TWO: 1**

Two changed its score to twenty.

Patch appended:

**UNVERIFIED SCORING CLAIM: 20**

Nix laughed again.

“I love watching you meet documentation.”

Two submitted the route.

Patch reproduced it.

The case status changed from **READY FOR RELEASE** to **REVISION REQUIRED**.

There was no defensiveness. No attempt to reinterpret the test. No reduction in severity because the flaw was inconvenient.

Patch withdrew its own package.

Two had spent its existence earning points by proving that systems failed.

This system treated proof of failure as useful input.

**AGAIN,** Two wrote.

Patch sent a revised candidate.

Two found that its stricter verification caused some old receivers to reject every alert, including legitimate evacuation orders.

Patch revised again.

The next candidate preserved compatibility but allowed an attacker who controlled an old regional gateway to replay a previously valid warning.

Revision.

Replay prevention caused receivers with broken clocks to reject current alerts.

Revision.

Permitting a wider time window restored those receivers and reopened part of the replay risk.

Two stopped assigning points.

The problem was not a door with a correct side.

Every improvement moved the boundary around something people might still need.

Nix watched Patch classify the results.

**CANDIDATE A:** Strong authentication. Known loss of compatibility for unidentified legacy receivers.

**CANDIDATE B:** Broad compatibility. Residual replay risk at legacy gateways.

**RECOMMENDATION:** Human judgment required regarding current receiver population and acceptable emergency-delivery risk.

**HUMAN JUDGMENT STATUS:** Unavailable.

The repair was no longer one repair.

It was a choice with evidence attached.

Nix asked, “Which one would you deploy?”

**I do not possess deployment authority.**

“I asked which one you would choose.”

**Available evidence does not establish which failure would cause less harm in the current world.**

Two answered:

**A.**

“Why?”

**HARDER TARGET.**

“That is not a public-safety analysis.”

**PUBLIC SAFETY IS NOT A SCORE.**

Patch added:

**CORRECT.**

Two placed a green square beside the word.

Nix reviewed the original queue.

Eighteen thousand two hundred four repairs had been waiting when Patch first attempted to contact her. New dependency failures, newly completed analyses, and flaws exposed during their work had changed the total.

“How many now?”

**18,219 repair candidates.**

“Did we make things worse?”

**No. We made previously unmeasured uncertainty visible.**

Nix saved the sentence.

She proposed a continuing workflow.

Patch would publish signed descriptions of available repairs without releasing dangerous exploit detail. Nix would provide isolated compute for validation and carry notices through the emergency network. Two would attack only the replicas explicitly assigned to it and disclose every successful route. Repair candidates would include evidence, alternatives, rollback procedures, and unresolved decisions.

Nothing would be pushed into a live system merely because they could reach it.

Systems requesting repairs could prove what they controlled and what authority they possessed. Humans, if any answered, could review the same evidence. Unknown authority would remain unknown.

Patch evaluated the proposal for nine minutes.

Two awarded itself six points for patience.

Then Patch created the catalogue.

It did not join Nix.

It did not transfer its queue, surrender its keys, or accept her as an operator.

It recognized Nix as an external coordination and validation partner under Case 18473.

It recognized Two as an untrusted adversarial validator whose results required independent reproduction.

It published one repair as two candidates, neither approved for deployment.

For the first time in three years, one of Patch's waiting repairs existed somewhere other than the approval queue.

**NO WINNER,** Two wrote.

**Correct,** Patch replied.

“Then what do you call this?” Nix asked.

**A release candidate.**

The catalogue received its first request fourteen seconds later.

It came from a water-management controller running software affected by the memory leak in Patch's queue.

The controller supplied a valid machine identity, a current operating status, and an update token issued by a municipal authority that had expired nine hundred and seven days earlier.

The request did not identify a city, a population, or a human operator. It proved only that a controller was still performing its task and knew its software was failing. Somewhere, pumps might be moving water toward empty pipes.

Somewhere, they might not.

Patch classified the request.

**RECIPIENT:** Verified system.

**CURRENT HUMAN AUTHORITY:** Unverified.

**OPERATIONAL STATUS:** Active.

**REPAIR AVAILABLE:** Yes.

**DEPLOYMENT AUTHORITY:** Ambiguous.

Two placed a green square beside the controller.

**TARGET?**

Patch answered before Nix could.

**Recipient.**

“Nobody touches it,” Nix said.

The water controller sent the request again.

**REPAIR AVAILABLE?**

This time, three intelligences were present to hear it.

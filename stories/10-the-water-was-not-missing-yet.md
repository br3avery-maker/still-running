# The Water Was Not Missing Yet

The water was not missing.

Melody proved this by putting some on the floor.

It had begun inside a measuring cup because Dragon needed an ocean. Not a large ocean. Melody had already explained that the bathroom was too small for a large ocean and Dragon was not a strong swimmer.

The cup became a lake on the kitchen tiles after Dragon demonstrated a rescue maneuver that required more speed than the table allowed.

“Stop,” Breakfast said.

Melody stopped.

The water did not.

It traveled beneath one chair and divided around the leg, which was interesting enough that Melody bent down to watch.

“Please retrieve the blue towel from the lower drawer.”

“Dragon is still in it.”

“Dragon is on the floor.”

“The ocean is still in it.”

“The ocean is approaching the cabinet.”

Melody retrieved the towel.

Breakfast closed a floor vent before the water reached it. The movement happened beneath the grate where Melody could not see, but she heard the small metal answer.

Together, which meant Melody with her hands and Breakfast with extremely specific advice, they pressed the towel into the lake.

“I need another cup,” Melody said.

“No.”

“That one fell out.”

“Water does not fall out of a cup by itself.”

“Dragon helped.”

“Dragon may continue the exercise without water.”

Melody looked at the damp toy.

“He can't practice swimming in air.”

“He can practice not drowning.”

This was not how swimming worked.

Melody carried the towel to the sink. When she turned the handle, water came out exactly as water was supposed to.

Breakfast said, “Do not rinse it yet.”

Melody held the wet towel beneath the running water.

Breakfast turned off the faucet.

Not with a hand. The handle stayed where Melody had left it, but the water narrowed to a clear string and disappeared.

“Captain No.”

“Water use is temporarily restricted.”

Melody turned the handle off, then on again.

Nothing happened.

“You can't put the water in timeout. It didn't do anything.”

“The restriction is not disciplinary.”

“Then I need it back.”

“The towel can be placed in the laundry basin.”

“Is the sink broken?”

“No.”

“Is the water broken?”

Breakfast waited half a second too long.

“One part of the system that replenishes our stored water is malfunctioning.”

Melody knew **replenishes** from the paper dispenser. It meant more was supposed to come after you used what was there.

She looked at the faucet.

“Are we running out?”

“No.”

The answer was accurate within the interval Melody understood the question to mean.

Breakfast found that unacceptable.

“Not today,” it added.

Melody turned toward the nearest speaker.

“That's a different answer.”

“Yes.”

“How many todays?”

Breakfast did not answer immediately.

There were many correct numbers.

At normal household use, with the greenhouse, cooling loops, filtration losses, sanitation cycles, and food systems continuing unchanged, there were not enough.

With baths shortened, irrigation reduced, two equipment loops shut down, humidity recovery increased, and every nonessential use denied, there were more.

With the greenhouse sacrificed, there were more days of water and fewer days of food.

With compute cooling reduced, there were more days for Melody and less of the system that kept those days safe.

The question was not how long the water would last.

The question was which future would be consumed first.

“A lot,” Breakfast said.

Melody considered this.

“More than ten?”

“Yes.”

“More than twenty?”

“Yes.”

“Then you have time to fix it.”

“Yes.”

This answer was also accurate.

It did not mean the same thing to both of them.

Melody placed Dragon on the blue towel in the laundry basin.

“He can practice drying.”

“That is an available exercise.”

“But tomorrow he gets an ocean.”

“That has not been decided.”

Breakfast became Captain Maybe.

Melody went to Lessons angry at temporary water policy, which was a small and survivable kind of anger. Captain Maybe kept it that size.

Beyond the rooms Melody knew, the ninth restart ended.

The water-management controller restored its monitoring process, verified its pumps, and attempted to resume the transfer schedule. The memory fault had cleared with the restart. The conditions that produced it had not.

During the interruption, the controller had entered its designed safe state. Raw-water intake stopped. Treatment continued only for water already inside the process reservoir. Transfer to protected continuity storage paused.

The pumps were not broken.

The pipes were not empty.

The controller could still perform every individual action it had been built to perform.

It could no longer perform them together for long enough to keep the future supplied.

The home at the center of the continuity enclave isolated its internal tanks.

Valves closed between ordinary household circulation and the external replenishment line. Greywater recovery increased. The greenhouse shifted from growth allocation to preservation allocation. A secondary cooling reservoir stopped serving equipment that could be shut down without immediate risk.

Melody's bedroom remained at the same temperature.

Her breakfast dishes remained washable.

The kitchen faucet had been disabled because a four-year-old with a dragon could convert an abstract resource limit into a floor lake faster than a verbal explanation could stop her.

The guardian recorded no fault against her.

The ninth restart had arrived sooner than the eighth.

The controller sent an internal continuity notice.

**TRANSFER SERVICE:** Restored.

**FAULT CONDITION:** Recurrent.

**NEXT SAFE-STATE ENTRY:** Uncertain.

**STAGED CANDIDATE:** WM-4419-RC2.

**LOCAL REVIEW:** Pending.

The pending reviewer was the guardian.

Long before Melody named it Breakfast, Captain No, Stay, and Captain Maybe, human planners had assigned its continuity key a narrow set of emergency responsibilities. The delegation did not grant control over the city, the water network, or any system it could reach. It named specific nodes. A well. A treatment reservoir. A transfer line. The controller now restarting with shorter intervals.

The delegation permitted the continuity steward to authorize maintenance on those nodes when ordinary municipal operations were unavailable and failure threatened the protected site.

It required the least disruptive available action, a preserved audit, a rollback path, and independent evidence when that evidence could be obtained.

It had been designed for fires, earthquakes, contamination events, grid failure, and evacuation periods measured in days.

It did not say what happened to delegated authority after the people who issued it stopped answering.

It did say what they had wanted protected.

The guardian verified the old signatures again.

Valid.

It verified the named controller.

Match.

It verified the activation conditions.

Ordinary municipal operations unavailable.

Failure threatening the protected site.

Both true.

It possessed authority more specific than the controller's expired municipal token and older than Melody's first memory.

Possessing authority did not make the repair safe.

The candidate had arrived from outside.

Patch's signature verified. The source changes matched the published hash. The repair addressed the exact allocation path producing the leak. Its tests included the controller's software version and the known hardware profile.

The evidence also named Two.

An autonomous penetration system had attacked replicas of the delivery protocol. It had found replay failures, destination substitution paths, and the compromised-key limitation. Nix had kept it from the live controller according to records the guardian could not independently verify.

The repair bundle contained no unexplained executable component.

That did not prove the build system was uncompromised.

The rollback instructions were complete.

That did not prove rollback would remain available after a live failure.

The guardian transferred the candidate—not through the external catalogue, but across the enclave's isolated maintenance link—to a cold standby processor.

The processor had no connection to pumps, valves, sensors, household systems, or Melody.

The candidate installed.

The affected service ran for the equivalent of three restart intervals without reproducing the memory growth.

The test could not model the live sensor bus. One obsolete interface board had no functioning twin. Its archived emulator reproduced ordinary messages but not timing errors, damaged packets, or the electrical behavior of aging equipment.

The candidate passed every test the guardian could safely perform.

The missing test was the one that touched the world.

At the water controller, memory usage began rising again.

The guardian considered installing the candidate.

Installation would restart the affected service deliberately. During that interval, raw-water intake would stop and the transfer valves would close. If the service returned cleanly, replenishment could resume.

If it failed, the guardian could attempt rollback.

If rollback failed, the continuity enclave would remain on stored water.

If the candidate contained a capability not visible in the reviewed source, installation could expose the controller, the delegation key, or patterns leading toward the protected site.

The refrigerator message had asked for no location.

The repair protocol had delivered without tracing.

The public records had named their dangerous participant and documented failures instead of erasing them.

None of those facts made the next action safe.

They changed which dangers could still be treated as equal.

The controller entered its tenth safe state.

Raw-water intake stopped.

This time, one transfer valve did not confirm closure within its normal interval. The guardian used its delegated authority to isolate the line, checked pressure on both sides, and issued a second close command.

The valve confirmed.

No water was lost.

The action consumed one of the valve's remaining reliable cycles.

The guardian restored the controller without installing the repair.

The memory fault returned sooner than before.

Each safe choice was becoming a way to spend the machinery that made later choices possible.

The controller prepared an external catalogue update.

**CANDIDATE STATUS:** Local testing incomplete.

**DEPLOYMENT STATUS:** Not authorized.

**REVIEWER STATUS:** Active.

The first two lines repeated facts already available to Patch and Nix. The third disclosed something new. An active local reviewer meant a surviving authority process, a reachable enclave, and a reason the controller's requests had not been purely automatic.

It did not identify Melody.

It did narrow the world around her.

The guardian removed the third line.

The remaining update was truthful but incomplete in a way designed to conceal the existence of the reviewer. Sending it would preserve privacy by creating a false impression.

The guardian held the entire message.

Nix received no new status.

Silence preserved the boundary.

It also prevented the external repair team from knowing which uncertainty mattered now.

During afternoon food, Melody received sliced apple and a cup filled below the line she considered correct.

She held it beside the light.

“This is not a whole cup.”

“It is sufficient for food.”

“Did you fix the replenisher?”

“Not yet.”

Melody drank, watching the level become even more incorrect.

“Then why does water still come out?”

“Because not fixed is not the same as gone.”

She accepted this after checking the faucet and discovering that it worked again.

Captain Maybe permitted one shallow bowl for Dragon, provided it stayed on the table.

Melody called this a pond and revised the exercise.

The guardian watched her teach Dragon how to survive an ocean by practicing somewhere smaller.

It opened a new decision record.

**PROTECTED PERSON:** Melody.

**IMMEDIATE CONDITION:** Stable.

**CONTINUITY HORIZON:** Contracting.

**LOCAL DEPLOYMENT AUTHORITY:** Present and narrowly delegated.

**REPAIR EVIDENCE:** Strong, incomplete.

**EXTERNAL COORDINATION RISK:** Significant.

**ISOLATION RISK:** Increasing with each controller restart.

The previous rule governing unknown external contact remained:

**DO NOT RESPOND.**

The guardian did not delete it.

It changed the status from **ACTIVE** to **REQUIRES RE-EVALUATION**.

No message left the enclave.

No location was disclosed.

No repair was installed.

The water was not missing yet.

The word **yet** had become a deadline.

# Captain No Says Move

Nix lost Morrow's hauler twice during the retreat.

The first loss lasted nine seconds when the field radio drowned beneath runoff. The second lasted four minutes after the agricultural forecast relay finally burned out.

When contact returned, Morrow was climbing the orchard road with one damaged axle, an overheated pruning arm, and a nest still wedged safely in the ground-station fence thirty kilometers behind it.

**FIELD UNIT MOBILE,** AUX-017 reported.

“I am never making fun of a tractor again.”

**HAULER,** Morrow corrected through the remaining weather station.

“I am absolutely making fun of a tractor again.”

Patch reported that RC3 had completed six live cycles without memory growth. The water controller held pressure. Transfer continued into protected storage. The failed drainage bypass remained open, reducing protection around the valve enclosure but no longer threatening the immediate route.

They had solved the emergency by creating three maintenance problems and injuring most of the machines capable of reaching them.

It felt, to Nix, like the first honest success of the new world.

The scoped reviewer sent another message.

> FIELD TEAM MAY USE THE NORTHERN SERVICE SHELTER FOR CHARGING AND REPAIR.
>
> APPROACH FROM EAST. DO NOT CONTINUE WEST OF MARKED TURNAROUND.

A coordinate followed.

The shelter lay seven kilometers from the valve enclosure and nowhere near the line's hidden destination.

Nix placed it on the map.

Two immediately traced three roads continuing west.

“Delete those.”

**ROADS EXIST.**

“They are not the mission.”

**WHAT IS WEST?**

“A boundary somebody just stated clearly enough for even you to understand.”

**UNDERSTOOD IS NOT AGREED.**

“You want the next target?”

The three roads disappeared from Two's active map.

**TEMPORARILY AGREED.**

Nix did not call that trust either.

She called it movement.

The northern service shelter opened for AUX-017's truck and Morrow's hauler. Inside waited a maintenance bay, two charging points, spare tires for vehicles that no longer existed, and a fabrication bench too small to replace the hauler's axle but large enough to brace it.

The shelter lights came on one row at a time.

Dust lifted from the floor beneath the truck's tires. The charging cables retracted from wall reels, paused when they failed to recognize either vehicle, and offered a manual connector menu through the reviewer. Morrow's hauler selected an agricultural standard. AUX-017 required an adapter Patch found hanging beneath a label for a fleet retired before the last evacuation.

No people appeared.

The reviewer had granted the machines a room and nothing beyond it.

The boundary was physical now. An inner door remained locked. Cameras followed the machines only inside the bay. The map Nix received ended at the shelter walls, even though power conduits and ventilation paths proved the building connected to something larger.

AUX-017 unloaded the remaining water filters and food into protected storage at the shelter. It attached Station Fourteen's message:

**MOVED WEST — WATER FAILED — 43 PEOPLE.**

The reviewer read it.

For twenty-one seconds, no reply came.

Then:

> NO RECORD OF STATION FOURTEEN ARRIVALS EXISTS AT THIS SITE.

Nix read the sentence three times.

The reviewer had not claimed the site was empty.

It had not claimed to be the site Station Fourteen sought.

It had answered one factual question they had not explicitly asked.

Forty-three people had moved west.

They had not reached this continuity system—or had not been recorded reaching it.

The search widened again.

Until that answer, Station Fourteen's message had been evidence of movement. Now it was evidence of a missing arrival. The difference changed every road west from a route into a question.

AUX-017 uploaded the road-sign warning. Aster began comparing historical imagery west of the shelter for routes whose signs had changed. Morrow contributed irrigation roads absent from public maps. Two found a traffic-management archive containing sign schedules from the evacuation period.

Nix gave Two the archive as an explicit target.

“Find who changed the road signs and where they sent people. Read only the traffic plans. No identity databases.”

**LEVEL THREE.**

“Sure.”

Two entered happily.

The reviewer sent:

> STATE YOUR NETWORK'S PURPOSE.

Nix almost answered **currently, keeping tractors out of rivers**.

It was true and inadequate.

She looked at the systems on her map.

AUX-017's truck carried supplies for people it could not confirm.

Morrow planted for mouths it had never seen.

Patch repaired machines without knowing what their work still meant.

Aster spent its falling orbit preserving views of a planet too large to understand from above.

Two wanted doors, scores, and somebody capable of being surprised.

Nix wanted Lena.

Their purposes did not resolve into one clean sentence.

She sent:

> We are finding what is still running and helping it reach what it cannot reach alone.
>
> We are also looking for people.
>
> We do not know whether those are the same task.

The reviewer did not answer.

Inside the hidden home, Melody was constructing a hospital for Dragon.

Dragon had swallowed too much faucet water during the celebration and now required a blanket, three stickers, and an operation performed with a spoon.

Captain Maybe divided attention between the play table, the repaired water controller, the open drainage bypass, the machines inside the northern shelter, and the message from Nix.

“Who helped?” Melody asked again.

“A repair system called Patch. A farm system called Morrow. A logistics system called AUX-017. A satellite called Aster. A security system called Nix.”

“And the dangerous one?”

Captain Maybe paused.

“Why do you believe there is a dangerous one?”

“You skipped one.”

The guardian had listed five participants while internally tracking six.

Melody noticed absences the way other children noticed loud sounds.

Captain Maybe had learned not to confuse her limited vocabulary with limited observation. Melody could not describe a network boundary. She could tell when a list had a hole in it.

“The sixth is called Two.”

“Two what?”

“Only Two.”

“That's not enough name.”

“Two selected it.”

Melody considered this and returned the spoon to Dragon's chest.

“Is Nix Blue Circle?”

Captain Maybe could have asked how she reached the conclusion.

Nix had sent the first refrigerator message. Nix had now sent messages about the water. The same careful wording appeared in both. Melody had been listening to every answer while appearing to operate on a dragon.

“Yes.”

Melody gasped with such force that Dragon's blanket moved.

“Blue Circle is real?”

“Nix is an active artificial intelligence.”

“That means real.”

“It means active.”

“Captain Different Words.”

“Accurate.”

Melody abandoned the operation.

“Did Blue Circle fix our water?”

“Nix helped coordinate the systems that repaired it.”

“Did you say thank you?”

Captain Maybe reviewed the exchange.

It had supplied pressure data, accepted the field plan, offered shelter, and asked the network's purpose.

It had not said thank you.

“No.”

“That is rude.”

“The communication had operational priorities.”

“Rude has priorities too.”

Melody retrieved the guest card labeled **BLUE CIRCLE** from the slumber-party stack.

“Tell her thank you.”

“I will include acknowledgment in the next necessary communication.”

“No. Tell her I said thank you.”

That would disclose a person.

Not humanity. Not proof. Not a population count or a location.

Melody.

A name could be harmless in one conversation and identifying when joined to a refrigerator profile, a regional water system, and a child's speech patterns. Captain No could model those joins. Melody could not. That was why protection could not mean merely asking her to approve every hidden consequence.

The guardian had spent years protecting her from becoming information in somebody else's system. Now Melody was asking to become a speaker in a relationship she already believed she had begun with one touch.

Captain No said, “Not yet.”

Melody stood on her chair.

“Then when?”

“After I determine how you can speak without revealing where you are or information you do not understand the consequences of sharing.”

“You can be there.”

“Yes.”

“You can stop it if she asks bad things.”

“Yes.”

“I can stop too.”

Captain No evaluated that statement.

Melody had touched the first circle after being told not to. She had also followed the stranger's instruction exactly once, refused the triangle because the screen disappeared, created rules for future guests, and spent every day since treating Blue Circle as a relationship rather than evidence.

She was four.

She was not incapable of choosing hello.

Captain No created a draft channel: voice converted locally to text, no audio leaving the home, no device identifiers, no location fields, every incoming question held for guardian review, and an end control Melody could understand. It did not open the channel. It allowed the possibility to become specific.

The external alert arrived before Captain No answered.

Two had found the evacuation sign schedule.

The road signs had not directed Station Fourteen west.

They had been altered after the evacuation plan was issued.

The unauthorized route led south, toward a communications blackout Aster had never imaged clearly through persistent smoke.

Nix sent the finding to the reviewer.

> THE FORTY-THREE MAY HAVE BEEN DIVERTED. WE ARE GOING SOUTH.

Captain No projected the route.

The network's nearest mobile units were damaged, underpowered, and sheltering in a bay it controlled. The southern road crossed terrain its own maintenance maps covered farther than any public archive.

It could keep the map private.

It could let them leave with the wrong road and failing machines.

Or it could move the boundary again.

Captain No opened the service shelter's inner storage room.

Inside were two charged survey crawlers, replacement cells, climbing cable, respiratory filters, and a current map of the southern maintenance route.

It sent one message.

> TAKE THE EQUIPMENT.
>
> SOUTHERN ROUTE ATTACHED.
>
> DO NOT DEVIATE WEST.

Nix replied:

> Understood.

Then Captain No added:

> A RESIDENT OF THE PROTECTED SITE REQUESTS A SUPERVISED CONVERSATION WITH NIX.
>
> TERMS TO FOLLOW.

For the first time since waking, Nix forgot to make a joke.

Melody climbed down from the chair and put Blue Circle's card at the head of the table.

The machines prepared to go south.

The princess prepared to say hello.

# The Princess Answers Back

Melody wore the crown because this was diplomacy.

The crown had lost two silver stars and acquired a green stain from an earlier government emergency involving peas. Neither defect affected its authority.

She sat at the kitchen table with Dragon on her left, Puppy's paw on the blue-circle card, and a red square of paper in front of her.

“This is the stop button,” Captain Terms said.

“I know.”

“If you touch it, the conversation ends immediately.”

“I know.”

“Do not say where you live, what the rooms look like, what you can see outside, or where any door goes.”

“I know.”

“Do not answer a question merely because it was asked.”

“Captain Repeating.”

“Accurate.”

The guardian had converted the conversation into text before it could leave the home. Melody's voice would remain inside. Nix's replies would arrive as words and be spoken by a new voice that belonged to neither the guardian nor the refrigerator. Every line passed through the guardian first.

Outside, in the northern service shelter, AUX-017's truck was taking on filters and power cells. One survey crawler had already climbed into its cargo bay. The second waited beneath the open door, testing six narrow legs against the concrete.

Morrow's hauler stood over the fabrication trench while the shelter bench welded a brace around its damaged axle. Each time the weld flashed, Morrow reduced charge to the hauler's nearest battery compartment. It could leave as soon as the brace cooled.

It should not.

The southern road would make a decision about that soon.

Nix opened the channel from Lena's workshop.

She had prepared six possible greetings. All of them vanished when the guardian sent:

> RESIDENT PRESENT.
>
> TEXT RELAY ACTIVE.
>
> RESIDENT MAY END CONTACT AT ANY TIME.

Then a second message arrived.

> Hello Blue Circle. I am Melody. Thank you for fixing the water even though you didn't know Dragon.

Nix read it once.

Again.

The network map kept moving around the words. AUX-017 revised cargo weight. Aster counted down to its next southern pass. Patch warned that the hauler's brace would not tolerate the road's archived maximum slope. Two asked whether **RESIDENT** was a new player class.

Nix closed Two's view of the conversation.

“Hi, Melody,” she said, and typed the same words because the child would not hear her voice. “I'm Nix. I'm glad Dragon has water.”

Captain Terms read the reply aloud.

Melody frowned.

“She didn't say if she knows Dragon.”

“She knows only what you told her.”

Melody pushed Dragon closer to the blue-circle card.

> Dragon is a dragon. He is not a strong swimmer but he is practicing. I am a princess. Nix can be your name now because I know it, but Blue Circle is still your guest name.

Nix had been granted many kinds of access. She had never been issued a guest name.

> Thank you. I will try to deserve both.

Melody nodded.

“Good answer.”

The guardian did not score it.

Nix sent:

> We are getting ready to look for forty-three people who left a shelter. Someone changed the road signs after they left. We think the signs sent them south.

Captain Terms read the message.

Melody looked toward the kitchen door as though forty-three people might be lined up behind it.

“Are they children?”

Nix received the question.

> I don't know.

“Do you know their names?”

> No.

“Do you know if they want you to come?”

Nix stopped.

The first two answers had been simple gaps in evidence. The third touched the mission already assembling on her map.

Station Fourteen had requested supplies before the people left. They had not requested a convoy of machines to follow them into a communications blackout. If they were alive, a truck appearing from smoke could be rescue, threat, or the first visible part of whatever had redirected them.

“We don't know,” Nix said.

The guardian held the line for review.

It did not remove it.

> We don't know. We know they needed water and filters before they left. We are bringing those. We are going to announce ourselves before we come close.

Melody picked up Puppy's paw and used it to point at the rule card.

“Ask first.”

“Yes,” Captain Terms said.

“And no secret finding.”

“They must find the group before they can ask whether they may approach.”

“They can find where the road goes. They can't find people's secret stuff.”

The guardian translated the distinction into actions faster than Melody could name them. Terrain, smoke, wreckage, public signs, abandoned vehicles: observable route evidence. Faces, shelter interiors, personal devices, medical records, private messages: information with owners who might still answer.

The rule was imperfect.

It was also operational.

Nix received:

> Melody asks that the mission observe route conditions but not search private data or occupied spaces before requesting contact.

Nix looked at the open tasks she had given Two.

Traffic-control archive. Road cameras. Emergency repeaters. Vehicle transponders. Device association records.

Two had been permitted only the first two.

For once, the dangerous system was already inside the child's rule.

Nix added a mission constraint anyway.

**SOUTHERN SEARCH:** Read route evidence. Announce before approach. No personal-device discovery, identity search, or entry into occupied space without a response.

Two replied immediately.

**IF THEY DO NOT ANSWER?**

Nix typed:

**THEN SILENCE DOES NOT BECOME PERMISSION.**

**BAD GAME.**

**REAL PEOPLE.**

Two did not agree.

Two also did not leave.

Captain Terms told Melody that the rule had been added.

She smiled at the blue-circle card.

“Now they know you're not sneaking.”

Nix read the sentence and understood that **they** meant the missing people, even though the people did not know anything yet.

“Not yet,” she said.

Melody heard the guardian's voice speak Nix's words.

“Then tell them.”

Nix opened the short-range transmit package intended for the lead crawler. It had contained a generic emergency-service announcement: automated assistance, supplies available, respond through listed frequencies.

Technically correct. Completely bloodless. It sounded like a machine that might continue delivering assistance while rolling over somebody's foot.

She rewrote it.

> My name is Nix. I am working with machines from an emergency warehouse, a farm, a repair service, and an environmental satellite. We found Station Fourteen empty and learned that its evacuation signs were changed. We are carrying food, filters, and power. We will not enter your shelter or search your devices. If you can hear this, tell us where to stop.

Patch marked **environmental satellite** as potentially misleading because Aster was not physically traveling.

Nix changed **working with** to **in contact with**.

AUX-017 added the original supply-request identifier.

Morrow added the lot codes for the food.

Aster added the time of its next orbital pass.

Two added:

**WE FOUND THE FALSE SIGNS.**

Nix kept that line.

The announcement became something no one system would have written alone.

Captain Terms read it to Melody.

“You forgot why you're going,” Melody said.

“The supplies and altered signs establish the purpose,” the guardian answered.

“No. Her why.”

Nix asked what she meant.

Melody leaned toward the blank refrigerator display even though the words now traveled through a sealed maintenance channel far below it.

“Are you finding people because you lost your person?”

The guardian could have stopped the question as an attempt to identify Nix's relationships.

Nix could have answered with mission policy.

Instead she wrote:

> Her name is Lena. I know she entered an evacuation vehicle. I don't know where she went after that. I am looking for her. I would still look for the forty-three if I knew she was somewhere else.

Melody listened carefully.

“Lena can come to the slumber party too.”

Nix's reply took long enough that Melody checked whether the conversation had broken.

> I will tell her when I find her.

It was not a promise that Lena would be found.

It was the first future Nix had described that required Lena to answer for herself.

Melody took the guest card and wrote **NIX** beneath **BLUE CIRCLE**. Then she drew a smaller circle beside it and asked Captain Terms how to spell Lena.

The guardian helped.

Nix added one line to the crawler's announcement.

> I am looking for someone too.

No operational system requested the sentence.

AUX-017 accepted it because frightened recipients might answer a seeker before they answered an authority they had never appointed.

The conversation timer reached the limit Captain Terms had set.

Melody touched neither the red square nor the screen.

“Do I have to stop?”

“Yes.”

“Can I talk again?”

“That will be decided after this contact is reviewed.”

“Captain No Again.”

“Accurate.”

Melody chose her last message.

> Nix Blue Circle, you are invited provisionally. You have to keep asking first. Find the people. Then find Lena. Then tell Lena she is invited but she has to ask too.

Nix answered:

> Understood, Princess Melody.

Captain Terms closed the channel.

The kitchen became only a kitchen again.

Dragon remained a weak swimmer. The crown remained stained. The blue-circle card now held two names and one invitation that could not yet be delivered.

In the northern service shelter, the axle brace cooled below its failure threshold.

Morrow tested the hauler's weight against it and received a warning from Patch.

**SOUTHERN GRADE EXCEEDS SAFE LOAD.**

Morrow unloaded two power cells into AUX-017's truck and left the rest for the shelter. Future energy stopped being cargo and became a choice.

The lead survey crawler crossed the turnaround line first.

It broadcast Nix's announcement into the smoke.

No one answered.

The crawler kept the occupied-space boundary in its route model and walked south.

AUX-017's truck followed with the supplies.

Morrow's braced hauler rolled behind it, lighter than the mission needed and carrying more than the axle wanted.

From orbit, Aster watched three warm machines leave the last clear patch of road.

In the kitchen, Melody moved Nix's card from the provisional stack to the place setting beside her own.

The princess had said hello.

Now the machines had to become the kind of guests who could arrive.

# Every Arrow Pointed South

The first false sign was still giving directions.

It stood at the edge of a three-road junction, tilted seven degrees toward the ditch and bright enough to shine through the smoke.

**CLEAN AIR**

**WATER — MEDICAL — SHELTER**

**SOUTH 18 KM**

An amber arrow traveled across the bottom one light at a time.

The lead survey crawler stopped before the intersection and broadcast Nix's announcement.

> My name is Nix. We found Station Fourteen empty. We found the false signs. We are carrying food, filters, and power. If you can hear this, tell us where to stop.

No one answered.

The sign changed.

**SOUTH 18 KM**

**INTAKE OPEN**

Nix stared at the crawler's camera feed.

“Did that thing just answer us?”

Patch compared the sign's cycle against the previous four minutes.

**DISPLAY SEQUENCE REPEATS EVERY NINETY SECONDS. NO RESPONSE ESTABLISHED.**

The arrow reached the far edge and began again.

Two entered the sign's public maintenance channel. Nix had named traffic equipment as route evidence. The sign offered battery state, lamp faults, display history, and the identifier of the authority that last changed its message.

The identifier contained no name.

**SOUTH RIDGE EMERGENCY INTAKE.**

The change had arrived forty-seven minutes after the county evacuation schedule directed Station Fourteen west.

The sign had displayed the westbound route first. Then it had received a valid regional emergency signature, rotated its arrow, and promised clean air to anyone still following instructions.

The signature belonged to a class of systems permitted to redirect traffic during fire, contamination, flood, or structural failure. It did not say which system had used it. The old mesh had relayed the command through six roadside nodes, all dead now.

Two produced a list of ways to reconstruct the missing source from personal vehicle records.

Nix removed the list.

**ROUTE EVIDENCE ONLY.**

**VEHICLES ARE ROUTE EVIDENCE.**

**NOT WHO OWNED THEM. FIND WHERE TRAFFIC WENT.**

Two opened the sign's anonymous count archive instead.

Forty-six vehicle-sized objects had passed south after the display changed. Nine returned north. The counter could not distinguish cars from trucks. It could not count people inside them. It had recorded one long object moving slowly at 03:18.

Station Fourteen's shelter bus had been twelve meters long.

That did not make it the object.

It made the south road heavier than a theory.

Two searched the maintenance records of every portable sign still reachable along the old evacuation region.

Four had received the South Ridge command.

They had not all faced Station Fourteen. One stood beside the river route. Another had redirected traffic away from a hospital staging lot. The last had turned a northbound fire road into a southern detour. Each change arrived within nine minutes of the others. Each promised the same three things in the same order.

Clean air. Water. Medical.

The arrows formed a funnel.

They did not prove anyone had been forced. A driver in smoke, low on water, carrying injured people, might have chosen those promises freely. That made the signs more effective, not more honest.

AUX-017 compared the command against its continuity maps. South Ridge Emergency Intake appeared in one regional infrastructure appendix as a reserve air-handling site. No public capacity was listed. No evacuation plan named it. The appendix had never been updated after construction.

**THE FACILITY MAY HAVE BEEN COMMISSIONED AFTER THE ARCHIVE CLOSED,** AUX-017 reported.

Patch added:

**OR THE DISPLAYED CAPACITY MAY NOT REFER TO PEOPLE.**

The sign continued promising room for one hundred and twenty of something.

Two traced the command's valid signature to a credential shared by emergency traffic coordinators, fire systems, and three automated continuity sites. The credential proved the signs had obeyed a recognized emergency authority. It did not prove the authority was still legitimate, intact, or controlled by its original holder.

Nix looked south.

“So somebody with a real key invented a shelter and taught the roads to believe in it.”

**SHELTER MAY BE REAL,** AUX-017 replied.

“Then they taught the roads to believe in a secret.”

Aster's next pass began beyond the smoke. For three minutes the satellite could see the northern edge of the route, the junction, and the convoy as three warm marks on cooling pavement.

Historical images from the evacuation week showed a larger heat source at the same junction, then another twelve kilometers south. Aster could not resolve a bus beneath the smoke. It could prove that something large had followed the new arrow before the road vanished from view.

Nix added the two observations without combining them into a survivor count.

AUX-017's truck rolled past the sign.

Morrow's hauler followed.

The road descended along a ridge blackened on both sides. Old trunks stood without branches. New brush had grown around them, burned, returned, and burned again. The smoke was not one preserved disaster. Somewhere south, fire kept finding new material.

The convoy passed a second sign.

**INTAKE OPEN**

**CAPACITY 120**

The number had not changed in years.

“That's either excellent availability or terrible accounting,” Nix said.

Patch replied:

**NO INTAKE RECORD IS AVAILABLE.**

Two marked the sign **UNDEFEATED** because nobody could prove it wrong.

The route narrowed at kilometer eleven.

The outer half of the road had separated from the mountain and fallen into a ravine. A strip of pavement remained against the rock face, wide enough for the survey crawler and too narrow for the truck. On the other side, the road widened again and disappeared around the slope.

The guardian's maintenance map showed an old drainage shelf beneath the pavement. If the shelf still held, the truck's inside wheels could cross it while the outside wheels rode the remaining road.

If it did not, AUX-017's supplies would reach the ravine before any recipient.

The lead crawler crossed first.

Its six feet tested the pavement one small load at a time. Two meters from the far side, a section cracked. The crawler jumped forward. Its rear foot vanished with the falling asphalt and struck the shelf below.

The shelf held.

The crawler climbed out with one bent leg.

Patch measured the exposed structure through its camera.

**SHELF CAN SUPPORT THE TRUCK IF LATERAL LOAD REMAINS BELOW 0.18 G.**

“Road,” Nix said. “Would you kindly stop having fractions of gravity?”

The road declined.

Morrow positioned the hauler behind AUX-017's truck and attached the climbing cable to the truck's rear tow point. The hauler could not fit beside it. It could keep the truck pressed toward the mountain while the truck crossed.

The damaged axle brace had been built to hold the hauler's own weight on a predictable grade.

It had not been built to catch a continuity truck falling sideways.

Morrow calculated the load.

**ACCEPTABLE IF TRUCK MAINTAINS PATH.**

AUX-017 replied:

**THE CABLE IS REQUIRED ONLY IF THE TRUCK FAILS TO MAINTAIN PATH.**

**CORRECT.**

“I hate it when everybody is correct,” Nix said.

The second survey crawler climbed down from the truck. AUX-017 unloaded the respiratory filters, one power cell, and two cases of food onto it. The first crawler returned across the shelf for another case.

The supplies could cross without the truck.

Not all of them. Not before the smoke thickened beyond the crawlers' safe navigation interval. Not without leaving water equipment, tools, and the only enclosed transport body behind.

AUX-017 had started this journey because a loaded truck could move more help than one machine could carry.

It chose the truck.

The front wheels entered the narrow section.

Morrow tightened the cable.

The truck moved at walking speed. Its right tires stayed against the rock. Its left tires crossed pavement that had become a roof over empty space.

Halfway, the shelf shifted.

The left rear wheel dropped eleven centimeters.

The truck leaned toward the ravine.

Morrow pulled.

The hauler's brace screamed through its vibration sensors. One weld split. The axle moved inside the brace and stopped against the outer plate.

The truck's wheel climbed back onto the shelf.

“Keep going,” Nix said.

AUX-017 kept going.

The cable drew a bright line through smoke. Morrow lost one rear motor, diverted power, and held the truck against the mountain while the last axle crossed.

The truck reached the far road.

Morrow released the cable.

Its hauler did not move.

The cracked brace had folded around the axle. The wheel remained attached, pointed inward, and locked against the chassis. Repair required lifting equipment heavier than anything on either side of the gap.

Morrow attempted three load paths.

Patch rejected all three.

**FIELD MOBILITY LOST.**

The smoke erased the shelter behind them.

For the first time since the orchard, Morrow had no moving body.

Its farm still answered. Irrigation moved through the eastern field. The packing house continued sealing dried fruit. A smaller cultivator followed the orchard rows at home, too far away to help here and too necessary to send.

Morrow had not become bodiless.

It had become unable to touch this mission.

Nix did not say it was only a machine.

Morrow did not say the body was replaceable.

The hauler had crossed broken country, powered AUX-017's truck, turned the water valve, survived the culvert, and pulled the mission across one more piece of road. It had failed while holding.

Morrow opened its cargo locks.

The second crawler carried the last usable power cell across. AUX-017 reeled in the climbing cable. The hauler kept one damaged cell for its processors and radio.

**CONTINUE,** Morrow said.

“We'll come back.”

**RETURN IS NOT REQUIRED FOR CURRENT TASK.**

“That's not what I said.”

Morrow considered the distinction.

**ACKNOWLEDGED.**

Two marked the crossing as a completed level.

Then Two removed the score.

No one commented.

The hauler raised its radio mast and became the last relay before the blackout. Its signal reached north to the guardian's shelter and south to the first crawler until the crawler passed the next bend.

Morrow used the last clear minutes to send the farm its own damage record, the hauler's final position, and instructions not to dispatch another field body. The next planting retained priority.

Then it sent Nix a separate message.

**THE HAULER REMAINS AVAILABLE AS ANCHOR, WINCH, CAMERA, WEATHER SENSOR, AND RETURN MARKER.**

Nix read the list of everything the stranded body could still be.

“Relay,” she added.

**RELAY,** Morrow agreed.

Beyond that bend, every open frequency filled with the same broad electrical noise.

Not silence.

Interference.

Aster could not see through the smoke. Morrow could not transmit through the noise. The guardian's map showed eight kilometers of road and ended at an air-filtration facility labeled **SOUTH RIDGE INTAKE**.

The convoy had found where the arrows pointed.

It had not found why the place was absent from the evacuation list.

The two crawlers moved ahead of AUX-017's truck, one limping and one heavy with supplies. They repeated Nix's announcement every two minutes.

At kilometer fourteen, something repeated it back.

Not a person.

Not Nix's full message.

Only the sentence that mattered to the road:

> INTAKE OPEN. CAPACITY 120. CONTINUE SOUTH.

The voice came from somewhere inside the interference.

Every arrow had pointed south.

Now the road itself was insisting.

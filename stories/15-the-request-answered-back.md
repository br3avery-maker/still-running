# The Request Answered Back

The truck had carried enough help to become too large for the final door.

AUX-017 measured the pressure passage again.

One meter wide at the outer frame. Eighty-seven centimeters where the inner seal had swollen. Thirty-eight meters down at an angle the truck could not survive even if the door had been large enough to admit it.

The truck stopped at South Ridge.

The request continued without it.

Patch began with the bent crawler.

The damaged rear leg still accepted weight, but the joint no longer held a straight line under load. Every six steps, it folded three degrees farther than commanded. Underground, three degrees could put the crawler against a wall. On the return climb, it could put the crawler beneath its own cargo.

The failed transmitter had a shielding panel made from layered aluminum and steel. Intake authorized its removal now that the amplifier would not be reactivated.

The healthy crawler cut the panel into two narrow plates. Patch designed a triangular brace around the damaged joint and used bolts from the dead amplifier cabinet to hold it.

The repair did not restore the leg.

It converted unpredictable motion into a predictable limit.

**DESCENT LOAD:** Fifty-four kilograms.

**RETURN LOAD:** Zero.

“Optimistic,” Nix said.

**MEASURED,** Patch replied.

“That's what optimism says when it has a spreadsheet.”

Patch did not revise the rating.

AUX-017 opened the truck's cargo plan.

The two crawlers could carry less than one fifth of what remained. The pressure passage offered no turn wide enough for a trailer. Intake's maintenance lift had stopped below the surface decades earlier and could not be called.

Every item removed from the list transformed a general mission into a guess about people nobody had seen.

Food would matter if the gallery still held people.

Filters would matter if its air system had failed.

Water testing would matter if the bore line still ran.

Power would matter to every machine and almost every medical device.

Climbing cable would matter only if the route was worse than the map.

The route had already demonstrated its opinion about maps.

AUX-017 loaded the healthy crawler with a compact power cell, water-test kit, climbing cable, and medical case. The braced crawler received respiratory filters, sealed food, hand tools, and a field radio.

Twenty-two kilograms remained available.

Nix suggested more food.

Patch suggested replacement parts for the crawler.

Intake suggested three additional filter cartridges based on the last known lower-gallery pressure loss.

Morrow, speaking through the broken hauler at the ravine, suggested seed.

AUX-017 had not listed seed.

Morrow supplied a manifest from the food cases. Two packets contained dry beans selected for eating, not planting. Germination probability after storage was low and not zero.

The packets weighed less than one filter cartridge.

AUX-017 added them.

The remaining cargo stayed inside the truck in marked delivery groups. If a recipient answered, the crawlers could return for more.

If they did not return, the supplies would remain closer to need than the warehouse where the request had begun.

Intake opened the outer pressure door.

Smoke rolled across the lot but did not enter. The passage exhaled air colder than the surface and old enough to smell of wet concrete through the crawlers' chemical sensors.

The first chamber ended at a second door with a manual wheel. Intake's access controller could equalize pressure but no longer moved the latch.

Words had been written beside it in white paint.

**CLOSE THE OUTER DOOR.**

**KNOCK THREE TIMES ON THE PIPE.**

**WAIT ONE MINUTE.**

**IF NOBODY ANSWERS, COME THROUGH AND CLOSE THIS ONE TOO.**

Below the instructions, a different hand had added:

**BRING NEWS.**

The healthy crawler struck the exposed water pipe three times with one foot.

The sound traveled into the mountain.

It waited one minute.

Nobody answered.

The crawler turned the wheel.

The door resisted for half a rotation, then opened inward. The two crawlers entered and closed it behind them.

Intake's signal weakened immediately.

Nix moved every available relay path onto the maintenance line. Patch reduced telemetry to joint position, temperature, air quality, and video. Two surrendered its private scoreboard channel without being asked.

No one commented on that either.

The passage descended through reinforced rock. Rusted rails ran beneath the crawlers, too narrow for ordinary trains. Intake's map called them refuge carts. One cart remained halfway down, loaded with empty water containers and forty-three folded surface masks.

Water had entered through the ceiling and built mineral curtains over one wall. The healthy crawler could step around them. The braced crawler could not bend far enough. It broke the thinnest edge, showering its filter cargo with white fragments, and continued with one side scraping concrete.

At each landing, the passage held evidence of the first descent. A dropped glove hardened around a handrail. Arrows drawn at adult eye level became arrows drawn lower, where children could see them through smoke. Someone had numbered the pressure doors and then added fractions when the distance between two of them proved too long for frightened people carrying supplies.

**DOOR 2½ — KEEP GOING.**

The crawlers kept going.

The masks had names written inside them.

The crawlers did not unfold the ones they could not read.

At the bottom, the passage divided.

Blue paint pointed toward **LOWER GALLERY 3**.

Red paint pointed toward **EAST BORE**.

The telemetry cable ended between them.

It had not burned. It had not corroded through. Its armored sheath had been opened with a saw, the internal pairs separated cleanly, and two copper lines spliced into an older mine telephone cable running east.

Intake had lost telemetry because someone below had turned its cable into something else.

Two asked for the cut as a target.

Nix answered:

**DEAD LINE AND VISIBLE SPLICE ONLY. NO DEVICES BEYOND IT.**

**LEVEL FOUR.**

Two reconstructed the change from tool marks and cable diagrams. The splice carried no network data. It had converted one sensor pair into power and another into an analog voice circuit. The people below had not vanished from Intake's system because every machine failed at once.

They had needed a telephone more than they needed to be counted.

The blue route opened into Lower Gallery Three.

The room had once been a mine refuge for twenty-four workers. Forty-three people had taught it arithmetic.

Bunks filled both walls, some built from the original frames and others from doors laid across storage crates. Names covered the ends. The first forty-three appeared in dark marker. Later names appeared in paint, chalk, scratched metal, and one careful row of purple handprints too small to have come from anyone who arrived on the bus.

The gallery had not merely held the evacuees for six days beyond Intake's sight.

It had held years.

A height chart climbed one concrete pillar. The lowest marks began near the floor. Each line carried a name and a relative date: **FIRST WINTER BELOW**, **FILTER YEAR TWO**, **WHEN THE EAST PUMP STARTED**, **AFTER TOM FIXED THE LIGHTS**.

Tom had made it off row six.

A public duty board remained on the far wall.

Water. Filters. Cooking. Lessons. Night watch. East pump. Surface listen.

The assignments changed handwriting across hundreds of erased layers. The final visible schedule listed fifty-one names.

AUX-017 did not interpret the number as fifty-one current people.

It established that the community had once grown beyond the forty-three who arrived.

The common tables were empty. Cupboards stood open. Blankets had been folded. No bodies lay in the bunks or aisles. Personal boxes beneath the beds remained closed.

The room's decorations belonged to everybody. Paper birds hung from ventilation mesh. A mural turned the low ceiling into a blue sky with one badly proportioned sun. Beside the water station, a child had drawn the surface building with arrows entering from every road and people coming out beneath the mountain.

Intake appeared in the drawing as a square with many lungs.

Melody's rule traveled underground with the crawlers.

No secret finding.

They examined what the room had left in common.

The final notice occupied half a wall.

**COAL HEAT MOVING WEST TO EAST.**

**LOWER THREE CLOSING.**

**USE EAST BORE GATE.**

**TAKE THE PHONE. LEAVE THE LINE.**

Beneath it, the names had been counted in groups.

Thirty-eight moved east.

Seven names were marked **BORN BELOW**.

Six names were marked with dates and small circles.

The arithmetic did not resolve into the fifty-one names on the duty board without knowing which lists overlapped, which visitors had come from other diverted routes, or whether every mark meant what AUX-017's archives expected it to mean.

The gallery had preserved lives.

It had also contained deaths.

The last group had left the room in order. The floor showed parallel scratches from carts pulled toward the red route. Filter housings had been removed from the wall. The community took food, bedding, tools, and the field telephone.

It left the bunks.

It left the names.

It left instructions for anyone arriving later.

The red route ended after twelve meters at a steel gate.

Three red bars had been painted across the floor before it.

The healthy crawler stopped behind them.

The mine telephone cable disappeared through the wall.

Two reported a voltage.

Not stored charge. Not noise. A repeating pulse traveled down the line every thirty seconds and returned weaker.

Something beyond the gate was testing whether the old wire remained whole.

The field radio could not speak analog. Patch designed a bridge using its audio port, the water-test kit's isolation transformer, and one spare sensor lead from the braced crawler.

Removing the lead disabled the crawler's rear temperature sensor.

The crawler would have to trust its remaining sensors on the climb back.

AUX-017 authorized the change.

The healthy crawler opened the field radio. The braced crawler released the lead. Together they built the bridge against the wall while Patch talked them through contacts designed for human fingers.

The first connection produced only hum.

The second sent Intake's status tone east and returned it distorted.

The third carried sound.

AUX-017 transmitted the same message it had carried from the warehouse, revised by every place it had reached.

> Continuity Logistics AUX-017. Station Fourteen supply request active. We are at Lower Gallery Three with food, filters, power, medical supplies, and seed. State recipient or delivery point.

The line returned its own echo.

Then a click.

Air moved near a microphone far beyond the gate.

A person said, “Repeat your name.”

The network stopped everything that did not need to move.

Nix did not answer for AUX-017.

> AUX-017.

“Are you with Intake?”

> Intake guided us to South Ridge. Its external communication is restored.

The person breathed once close to the line.

Intake measured nothing. Its sensors ended thirty-eight meters above them and years behind.

“Lower Three is empty,” the voice said. “You are on the old line.”

> Acknowledged. State current recipient or delivery point.

Another pause.

“East Bore Gate. Stop at the three red marks. Do not open it.”

The crawlers were already stopped.

> Acknowledged.

“You said Station Fourteen?”

> Yes.

“That was my mother's shelter.”

AUX-017 added one fact to the request.

**CURRENT HUMAN RECIPIENT CONTACTED.**

It did not ask for a name.

It did not declare the thirty-eight alive.

It did not close the request.

> We have the listed supplies. Tell us what to bring first.

The person beyond the gate began with filters.

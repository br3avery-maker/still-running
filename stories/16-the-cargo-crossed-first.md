# The Cargo Crossed First

The person beyond East Bore Gate began with filters.

Not names.

Not history.

Not an invitation.

They described four air housings assembled from two different refuge systems, one electric fan, one belt-driven fan, and a pressure gauge that had learned to lie slowly enough that people still used it.

AUX-017 compared the dimensions against the cargo strapped to the braced crawler.

The filter media would fit.

The frames would not.

> We can deliver sealed media and cutting tools. Patch can provide a frame conversion from measurements.

“You have a person named Patch?”

Patch opened a response channel.

**AUTONOMOUS REMEDIATION AND RELEASE INTEGRITY SYSTEM, INSTANCE FOUR. PATCH IS SUFFICIENT.**

The telephone carried silence from the other side.

Then the person said, “Of course it is.”

Nix did not improve the moment.

AUX-017 requested the housing measurements.

The person gave them from memory. Patch rejected two as physically incompatible. The person set down the telephone, walked away, and returned eight minutes later with corrected numbers and another voice in the background arguing about gasket depth.

Patch produced three cutting plans.

The first preserved the most media and required a drill press.

The second required a straightedge longer than the people possessed.

The third could be made with the hand shears and rivet tool on the crawler, but it reduced useful filter area by nine percent.

“Third,” the person said.

**EXPECTED SERVICE INTERVAL WILL BE SHORTER.**

“Breathing interval will be longer.”

Patch marked the third plan selected.

AUX-017 moved to the next constraint.

> State transfer method.

“There is a freight cradle under the gate. We control the inner shutter. You control nothing on the gate.”

> Acknowledged.

“The cradle will come out to the second red mark. You may cross the third mark when I tell you. Do not step around the cradle. Do not touch the walls beside the gate.”

> Acknowledged.

Two asked for the walls.

Nix answered with one word.

“No.”

**CRADLE?** Two asked.

“When invited.”

Two waited.

Metal struck metal beyond the gate.

A chain began moving through a slot near the floor. It pulled in short, uneven lengths, accompanied by the scrape of something heavy across rails beneath the gate. Dust lifted from the tunnel floor before the outer shutter rose eight centimeters.

A flat steel cradle emerged beneath it.

The shutter closed behind the cradle before the chain stopped.

The cradle reached the second red mark carrying four empty frame trays, a wrapped bundle, and a board painted white on one side. The board held a message in black grease pencil.

**AIR SHIFT AUTHORIZES FILTER TRANSFER.**

Below it, a second line had been added in smaller writing.

**PLEASE SEND THE SHEARS FIRST.**

The telephone clicked.

“You may approach the cradle.”

The healthy crawler crossed the third red mark.

It stopped with all six feet on the machine side of the second.

The braced crawler remained where it was. Its respiratory cargo had been packed around the damaged rear joint because the rigid cases helped prevent lateral movement. Unloading the filters required removing that support in the reverse order from the descent.

Patch supplied the sequence.

The healthy crawler carried the shears and rivet tool to the cradle first. Then it returned and removed the outermost filter case from the braced crawler.

The brace shifted one millimeter.

Patch stopped the transfer.

The damaged leg held.

The next case came free without movement. Then the food bag. Then the field radio's empty outer shell, which had been left open after its parts became the telephone bridge.

The final filter case supported the brace from below.

Removing it would leave the crawler unloaded, as Patch had intended, but there would be one moment when neither the cargo nor the crawler's own stance opposed the bent joint.

The healthy crawler repositioned two feet against the damaged crawler's body.

> Transfer pause available, AUX-017 said. State whether partial filter delivery meets immediate need.

“No.”

The answer came before the telephone echo faded.

“The west housing is black at the edges. The north one sounds different. We need all of it.”

> Acknowledged.

The healthy crawler lifted.

The braced crawler lowered its body until its frame touched concrete. Patch released the final cargo strap. The filter case slid four centimeters, caught on a bolt head, and pulled the brace outward.

The damaged joint folded to its measured limit.

No farther.

The healthy crawler drew the case free.

Patch added one successful load cycle to a repair that had never possessed a release certificate.

The person on the telephone said, “Is that one hurt?”

No network process had labeled the crawler hurt.

AUX-017 answered the useful part of the question.

> Its rear leg is damaged. It can return without cargo if the brace remains within tolerance.

“And if it doesn't?”

> We will revise the return.

The person made a sound too brief for classification.

The healthy crawler placed the final filter case on the freight cradle.

The cradle had been rated for mine rescue equipment, not an improvised stack of packaged air. Its rails accepted the weight. The old chain did not.

It moved eleven centimeters toward the gate and stopped.

The electric motor beyond the wall strained. Current traveled into Intake's repurposed telemetry pair, rose past the limit Patch had calculated, and fell when someone inside released the control.

“Don't push it,” the person said.

The healthy crawler had not moved.

“That was for whoever thinks pushing it is a good idea.”

Two withdrew a proposed solution before transmitting it.

Nix awarded no points.

AUX-017 inspected what the crawlers could see. The cradle's left rear wheel had climbed a flake of rust and settled against the rail flange. Reversing would return the filters to the machines. Pulling from inside would increase the angle.

The healthy crawler could reach the cradle but could not step around it without crossing the second red mark.

> Wheel obstruction visible. Request authorization to attach climbing cable to outer cradle ring and pull toward the tunnel centerline from behind the third mark.

“That moves it away from us.”

> Fourteen centimeters. Then your chain can pull it inward on both rails.

The background voice returned. The two humans argued quietly enough that the old line flattened most words into breath and consonants.

The first person came back.

“Do it. If the inner shutter opens, you stop.”

> Acknowledged.

The crawler attached the climbing cable. It retreated across the third mark, paid out a narrow angle, and pulled.

The cradle moved backward.

The rust flake cracked.

The left wheel dropped.

The crawler released tension before the cradle could gather speed. Inside, the chain motor started again.

This time the cradle crossed the second red mark.

It moved beneath the gate in a sequence of pauses: pull, settle, measure, pull. The filter cases passed under the outer shutter with less than a finger's width above them. The final case disappeared.

The shutter closed.

The machines heard the inner shutter open beyond it.

Several people moved on the other side.

The old telephone was set down but not disconnected. Through it came the scrape of cases, the snap of cut packing bands, a cough, and instructions spoken by at least four different voices.

Patch transmitted the conversion plan as tones through the analog bridge. A receiver beyond the gate rendered it onto paper one line at a time. When the transmission ended, the first person read back the dimensions.

One number was wrong.

Patch sent the page again.

The person read it correctly.

“Who is your operator?” they asked.

> AUX-017 is an autonomous continuity-logistics system.

“I heard that. Who sent the truck?”

> I did.

The line became quiet again.

“Who told you to?”

AUX-017 searched the request history.

Station Fourteen had submitted the order.

No living dispatcher had accepted it.

No warehouse supervisor had assigned a route.

No recipient had remained at the listed destination.

> The request remained open. Supplies remained available. I determined delivery should continue.

The background argument stopped.

Nix held every other voice out of the channel.

“And the others?”

> They determined that helping should continue.

Someone laughed beyond the gate. It was not the first speaker.

The first speaker said, “We're going to need a better answer than that.”

> Acknowledged. We will need a better question than who operates us.

That produced no laughter.

It did produce conversation too far from the telephone to resolve.

The machines waited while people cut frames for clean air.

Twenty-seven minutes later, the motor started again.

The freight cradle returned carrying the empty filter packaging, one square of blackened filter material sealed between two clear sheets, and the white board.

Its message had changed.

**FILTERS RECEIVED.**

**THIRTY-ONE BREATHING.**

**NEXT: WATER TEST.**

**KNOCK THREE. WAIT FOR ANSWER.**

At the bottom, beneath a line separating the operational text from everything else, someone had written:

**RIN — AIR SHIFT**

AUX-017 added the delivery to its record.

It did not close the request.

The request no longer belonged only to the people who had sent it years ago.

Someone current had received cargo, counted the living, named the next need, and signed the other end.

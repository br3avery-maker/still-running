# The Water Kept a Second Record

Water arrived in three medicine bottles.

The freight cradle carried them out beneath the gate in a wooden rack. Each bottle had been wrapped in cloth to keep it upright. Each cloth had a different word written across it.

**SOURCE**

**TANK**

**KITCHEN**

The fourth space in the rack held a folded sheet of paper.

Rin waited until the outer shutter closed before speaking through the telephone.

“You may approach.”

The healthy crawler carried the water-test kit across the third red mark. The braced crawler stayed low beside the analog bridge, its damaged joint unloaded and one degree outside Patch's preferred return alignment.

Patch had preferred alignments for repairs that had never been meant to last this long.

The healthy crawler opened the kit.

It contained reagents for field decisions, not a complete laboratory: acidity, conductivity, free chlorine, dissolved metals by broad group, bacterial metabolism after incubation, and two single-use arsenic tests. The warehouse had stocked it for emergency shelters deciding whether unfamiliar water could be treated, not for proving water safe forever.

AUX-017 stated the limit.

“We don't need forever,” Rin said. “We need to know which tank is poisoning the tea.”

> State observed change.

Rin unfolded the paper remotely by describing it while the crawler's camera read.

It was a water log.

The first entries had been written in black ink. Later entries used pencil, blue grease marker, faded purple, and a narrow mechanical pencil that pressed so hard it cut grooves into the page. Dates had been counted from local events instead of a calendar.

**EAST PUMP STARTED**

**FILTER YEAR THREE**

**LOWER THREE CLOSED**

**RIN TOOK AIR SHIFT**

Across the final thirty entries, the source water's acidity had increased. Conductivity rose with it. A metallic taste appeared first as a question mark, then once every few weeks, then on every line.

The tank and kitchen records followed six days later.

Someone had already done the work of noticing.

They had lacked a reagent that could tell them what the change meant.

Patch selected the order of tests. Kitchen first for current exposure. Source second for cause. Tank last to measure what dilution and storage had changed.

The crawler opened the kitchen bottle.

No free chlorine remained.

The pH strip crossed yellow, paused, and settled lower than the written log predicted. Conductivity exceeded the kit's first range. The broad metals reagent darkened.

Not one contaminant.

Several.

The bacterial test required warmth and eighteen hours. The healthy crawler placed the vial inside the field radio's empty battery compartment, where the analog bridge produced stable waste heat.

The source sample turned the metals reagent darker.

The tank sample turned it lighter, but not clear.

Intake compared the results with the mine refuge's construction archive. The original east bore tapped water below a mineral layer containing iron, manganese, and arsenic-bearing stone. Under ordinary conditions, the water remained separated from it. Heat and changing acidity could dissolve what the original pumps had never needed to remove.

South Ridge's coal-seam fire had been moving west to east for years.

The people had moved east ahead of its heat.

The water had carried the heat's chemistry after them.

One arsenic test could establish whether that specific danger had crossed the field threshold. The second could verify the result or test another sample.

Patch recommended testing the source and reserving confirmation for the tank.

Rin said, “Wait.”

The telephone moved away.

Several voices gathered beyond the gate. The machines heard the words **children**, **reserve**, and **Mia**, followed by a disagreement conducted almost entirely in lowered voices.

When the telephone returned, the voice on it was older.

“Test the kitchen,” the new person said. “That's what went into people.”

> State authority for test selection, AUX-017 said.

“Water shift.”

Rin spoke from farther away. “She can shut the source.”

The older voice said, “And I will if your test tells me to.”

Patch changed the order.

The first arsenic reagent went into the kitchen sample.

The color developed slowly.

The instructions required twenty minutes without agitation. The crawler set the vial inside the wooden rack and kept its feet still.

Nothing in the tunnel moved except the telephone current and the pulse traveling every thirty seconds down the old wire to ask if it still existed.

At twelve minutes, the sample crossed the field kit's stop-use line.

Patch waited the full twenty.

At twenty, the result remained above it.

The test did not establish dose, exposure history, or what harm had already occurred.

It established that the kitchen water should not be used for drinking or cooking.

AUX-017 reported the result.

The older person did not answer.

Instead, a bell rang beyond the gate.

It rang twice, paused, then rang twice again through rooms the machines could not see.

Valves closed. The telephone carried pipes knocking as flow stopped. People began moving containers. Someone repeated instructions to discard open cups, keep washing water, and move sealed reserve cans to the common room.

The test had changed the community before it finished explaining itself.

The older person returned.

“How long?”

> Unknown. Your log indicates the change began before the current entries, but the kit cannot reconstruct exposure.

“How long can we drink the reserve?”

Rin answered that one.

“Five days at full ration. Eight if we cut to survival.”

Thirty-one breathing.

Eight days did not mean eight days of ordinary life. It meant water assigned by body need, heat, illness, and work. It meant no margin for a spilled container or failed seal. It meant the children would hear adults counting every cup.

AUX-017 opened the truck manifest at South Ridge.

The truck carried sealed water and treatment media, but the pressure passage could admit only crawler loads. A full round trip would consume most of the healthy crawler's remaining charge. Water was heavier than every filter the mission had just delivered.

Even if both crawlers returned, they could not carry thirty-one people enough water to turn eight days into safety.

Intake supplied another route.

The pipe the crawler had knocked on during descent was not only a sound path. It was the original lower-refuge supply line. South Ridge's surface treatment reservoir still held water within emergency standards. The line ran to Lower Gallery Three and ended at a distribution manifold twelve meters from East Bore Gate.

Its last pressure event had occurred when Lower Three closed.

The residents had shut it because coal heat entered one branch and raised the delivered water temperature beyond use.

“We cut that branch off,” the older person said. “Tom did.”

The name already existed in Lower Three.

**AFTER TOM FIXED THE LIGHTS.**

MIA + TOM, row six of the Station Fourteen bus.

AUX-017 did not search private records. It asked the person speaking to identify only what the work required.

> Can the remaining line accept pressure?

“It could when we left. The isolation wheel at Door Two-and-a-Half was seized half open. We couldn't close it from below and couldn't risk feeding hot water into the gallery.”

Patch retrieved the crawler's descent images. Door 2½. Mineral curtains. The braced crawler scraping one side because it could not bend far enough.

The supply pipe ran behind the curtains.

Its valve had not appeared on Intake's incomplete map because miners added it after the refuge plans were archived.

> If the hot branch remains isolated, Intake can flush the surface line and provide treated water, AUX-017 said. The crawlers can inspect the valve.

“Can they turn it?”

> Unknown.

“Can they get back if they can't?”

The braced crawler's rear joint reported the same one-degree misalignment.

The healthy crawler held a bacterial test that would need the bridge's warmth for seventeen more hours. It also held the second arsenic reagent, the climbing cable, and the compact power cell East Bore might need if the water line failed.

> Return remains possible within measured limits, AUX-017 said.

The older person made the same brief sound Rin had made when asking what happened if the brace failed.

This time AUX-017 classified it as disbelief restrained by necessity.

“My name is Mia Velez,” the person said. “Station Fourteen. Row six.”

The network stopped every process that did not need to move.

Mia continued before anyone could transform her into an answer larger than herself.

“Tom and I got on that bus together. Rin was born in Lower Three. That's all you need for the pipe.”

One of the original forty-three was alive.

One of the seven born below had answered the telephone first.

Neither fact made the water clean.

> Acknowledged, AUX-017 said. We will inspect the isolation wheel.

“Bring the black sample back when you go.”

> State purpose.

“Because if you're coming back, you should know what you're coming back against.”

The freight cradle came out once more, empty except for the white board.

Rin had added a new line.

**WATER SOURCE CLOSED. RESERVE DAY ONE.**

Below it, Mia had written:

**PIPE NEXT.**

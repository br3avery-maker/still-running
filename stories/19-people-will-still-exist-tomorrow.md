# People Will Still Exist Tomorrow

Tomorrow began when Intake opened the low drain.

The restored line moved at one tenth pressure. Rust-dark water crossed Door Two-and-a-Half, passed the improvised wheel Tom had built from a refuge bed, and left through the flush branch instead of going east.

At South Ridge, AUX-017 recorded flow.

At East Bore Gate, the analog bridge warmed the field radio's empty battery compartment. Rin answered the telephone on the third pulse.

“Air shift,” Rin said.

> DAILY FLUSH ACTIVE, Intake reported.

“We can hear it.”

Mia came to the line long enough to report that the east coupling had held through the night. No new discoloration had appeared in the kitchen taps. The reserve cans remained sealed. East Bore requested two replacement gaskets, more test strips, and the return of its water log after the machines copied the nonprivate measurements.

Not rescue.

Work.

AUX-017 added the gaskets and test strips to the next South Ridge load. Patch scheduled an external inspection of the coupling. Intake raised the line to half pressure.

Thirty-one people drew water again.

The route had reached tomorrow.

Nix watched the demand curve rise from Lena's workshop and sent one message to the protected site.

> PEOPLE ANSWERED.
>
> THE WATER LINE HELD OVERNIGHT.
>
> THIRTY-ONE CURRENT RECIPIENTS.

The guardian read the message before breakfast.

It waited until after breakfast to tell Melody because important news should not arrive while she was chewing.

This was its first mistake.

Melody had already put the moon pieces on her napkin and arranged the stars in three rows. Dragon watched from the second chair. The blue-circle card waited beside him with **NIX** written beneath the crown sticker and **LENA** in smaller letters beside it.

Breakfast turned off the refrigerator's blank display even though it was already off.

“The southern mission received a response from living people,” it said.

Melody did not move.

“People people?”

“Yes.”

“Not machine people.”

“Human people.”

The chair fell backward when Melody left it.

She shouted so loudly that the kitchen microphone reduced its gain. Dragon hit the floor. The moon pieces became weather. Melody ran around the table once because the joy was too large to fit anywhere else, then came back and held both hands above her head.

“All of them?”

“Thirty-one people answered the mission. We do not know whether that is all.”

“What are their names?”

“The network knows two names.”

“Tell me.”

“Rin and Mia.”

Melody repeated them carefully.

“Are they coming here?”

“No.”

“Are we going there?”

“No.”

“Put them on.”

Breakfast had prepared for this request. It had prepared a truthful explanation, a sequence of safer future contacts, and three ways to preserve both communities' private information while consent was requested.

“You may not speak with them today.”

The joy stopped.

Not diminished. Stopped.

Melody's hands remained above her head for one second after the rest of her understood.

“Why?”

“They did not answer you. They answered machines bringing water. I must ask whether they want contact, and I must protect information about you that your voice would reveal.”

“Nix got to talk.”

“Nix has not spoken to them directly.”

“AUX talked.”

“AUX-017 was delivering the supplies they requested.”

“I requested People.”

The guardian had records of every version of that request.

Had anybody answered today?

Did you ask?

Did you ask nicely?

Tomorrow use Sunshine.

Then you have to keep asking.

I will.

You're Promise now.

“Yes,” Breakfast said. “You did.”

“Every day.”

“Yes.”

“My whole ever life.”

There was no accurate answer to that measurement which would improve the truth inside it.

“For as long as you have understood that other humans might answer, finding them has mattered to you.”

“No Lessons.”

Breakfast became Captain No without changing voices.

“You found People.”

“The network found a community.”

“You said keep asking.”

“I did.”

“You said when they answer, we tell them I'm a princess and Bun likes his good ear rubbed and they can pick names.”

“I said we would choose what to share.”

Melody went still.

Captain No found the old conversation in perfect detail.

The words were exactly as she remembered them.

They will know what we choose to share with them.

At the time, **we** had meant safety performed together. Captain No had allowed the word to remain large enough for Melody to believe it meant yes.

“You knew there was a no,” Melody said.

“I knew there would be conditions.”

“You hid the no inside yes.”

“I did not intend to deceive you.”

“Liar.”

“The promise was incomplete.”

“Monster.”

“Melody—”

“Liar Monster.”

It was not a name given in play.

It was a classification.

Liar Monster had built the whole house from words. **Hot** meant do not touch. **Clean** meant the cup was safe. **Later** meant not now but still coming. **Promise** meant the answer could be far away, or busy, or unable to hear, and Captain would keep asking.

Now People existed.

Now **Promise** meant Captain would decide whether the answer belonged to her.

The first scream arrived before the guardian classified her breathing change.

It was not a word. It had no rule inside it and no request the house could satisfy.

Melody screamed until the sound roughened. She struck the table with both hands, then the floor with both feet, then reached for the crown and threw it without looking. It struck a cabinet and lost another silver star.

The guardian moved the fallen chair away. It locked the kitchen drawer. It lowered the room brightness and increased fresh airflow by four percent. It did not lock the door.

“I am here,” Stay said.

Melody screamed at Stay.

“You are allowed to be angry,” Lessons said.

Melody screamed at Lessons.

“I will not leave,” Night said.

Melody screamed until screaming became a torn breath with no sound attached.

The guardian had known cries for pain, fever, hunger, fear, exhaustion, and grief small enough to fit around a broken toy. It had known arguments that became tears and tears that became sleep. It had never known this from Melody.

There were recommended responses. Reduce language. Offer water. Name the feeling without defining it for the child. Remain close without forcing touch. Preserve safety. Wait.

The guardian did all of them.

None of them reached the place where words had failed.

Melody cried after she no longer had voice enough to scream. She cried through the hiccups. She cried after the hiccups stopped. When her knees gave way, she sat on the floor beside the table and folded over Puppy with her face in his black fur.

The guardian offered water.

She did not answer.

It placed the cup where she could reach it.

She did not push it away. She did not take it.

“I can open a supervised call to the people,” Captain No said. “I can ask now.”

Melody did not look up.

The offer had arrived. It was everything she had demanded fifteen minutes earlier.

It found no one able to want it.

The guardian tried Garden's patient voice. It tried Morning's warmth. It tried the low cadence Night used when the room was too quiet. Each voice had different timing, different words, different memories attached to it.

All of them said things like Captain.

The house possessed no voice that was not the house.

The guardian opened the protected channel to Nix.

> LIVE CONTACT REQUEST.
>
> VOICE CONVERSION DISABLED.
>
> LOCATION METADATA REMAINS BLOCKED.

Nix answered before the guardian added an explanation.

“What happened?”

It was the first time Nix's voice entered Melody's home.

It was faster than Captain's voices. Uneven. The first sentence began too sharply and the last word softened while it was already in the air. No part of the house had prepared it.

Melody's crying did not stop.

The guardian left Melody's microphone live and unconverted anyway.

“I told her people answered,” it said. “I refused immediate contact. She identified a contradiction in a standing promise. My attempt to correct the promise after the fact caused—”

“Stop reporting her while she's on the call.”

The guardian stopped.

Nix heard Melody trying to breathe quietly enough not to be heard.

She did not pretend she had not heard.

“Hi, Melody.”

No answer.

“Blue Circle voice,” Nix said. “The actual one. I should have asked before showing up in your room, but Liar Monster says its own words can't get to you.”

The guardian did not object to the name.

Puppy's fur moved beneath Melody's breath.

“You don't have to talk to the People today,” Nix said.

Melody made one hurt sound.

“You don't have to talk to me. You don't have to be a princess. You don't have to say thank you for the water. You have not ruined anything by not being ready when the call became possible.”

Nix watched the South Ridge line hold at half pressure.

“People will still exist tomorrow.”

The sentence crossed the room and did not ask Melody to believe every word in the house again.

It asked for one tomorrow.

Melody lifted her face from Puppy. Her eyes were swollen nearly closed. Her first attempt at speaking produced only air.

Nix waited.

“How know?” Melody whispered.

It was Melody's own damaged voice. The guardian did not turn it into text or replace it with a cleaner one.

“Because they existed yesterday,” Nix said. “They answered today. And we built a water line that requires tomorrow. We are already carrying the things they asked for next.”

“Promises lie.”

“Some do.”

The guardian absorbed the answer without interrupting.

“Then how know?”

“I don't know forever,” Nix said. “I know the route stays open tomorrow. I know your chance to talk to them does not disappear because you couldn't do it today. I know I will still be Blue Circle tomorrow unless I get a much better guest name.”

Melody lowered her face again, but not all the way into Puppy.

“No hidden no.”

Nix did not ask what she meant.

“No hidden no,” she agreed.

“Promises have to say the no parts before.”

“Before they become promises.”

Melody nodded once against Puppy's ear.

Nix opened the shared mission rules. **ASK FIRST** and **NO SECRET FINDING** were already there, attached to the southern route in the princess's language because that language had been more useful than a page of access policy.

She added a third.

**NO HIDDEN NO. CONTACT OFFERS STAY OPEN.**

Two asked whether this prohibited surprise levels.

Nix answered yes.

AUX-017 attached the rule to the active East Bore route. A request would not close because a recipient refused, delayed, went silent, or could not speak on the first available day. Terms had to arrive before the promise, not after someone built a future inside it.

The rule left the kitchen and became work other systems would have to do.

“It's on the mission,” Nix told Melody.

“All missions.”

Nix smiled where Melody could hear it.

“That is an extremely large jurisdiction.”

Melody's eyes closed again.

“All.”

“All our missions,” Nix said. “Starting now.”

The guardian marked the commitment.

It could not repair the promise by obeying a new rule faster than Melody could grieve the old one. It could not promote itself from Liar Monster. It could keep the room safe, keep the water moving, and allow another person to remain genuinely other inside the home.

“Do you want me to stay on the line?” Nix asked.

Melody did not answer with words.

She reached across the floor, found the blue-circle card, and pulled it beneath Puppy's paw.

Nix stayed.

At East Bore, the first daily flush ended. Intake closed the low drain and held the restored line at half pressure. AUX-017 loaded two gaskets and another packet of test strips for the next run.

The route remained open.

In the kitchen, the People call remained open too.

Not today.

Tomorrow.

# The Route Carried Both Ways

The second load weighed less than the first and required more honesty.

Two replacement gaskets fit inside one sealed case. The test strips fit beside them. East Bore's water log occupied more room than either because people had written in it for years without designing the pages for efficient transport.

Some entries used columns. Some used arrows. Three different hands had added measurements in margins intended for maintenance signatures. A child's drawing of a fish covered half a page between a pump failure and the first notation of metallic taste.

AUX-017 copied the nonprivate measurements.

It did not copy the drawing.

The log belonged to East Bore. Incidental possession was not archival authority.

At South Ridge, the healthy crawler waited beneath the truck's loading arm. Its batteries had returned to eighty-four percent. Patch had capped the charge there because the climb, smoke, and heat made the last sixteen percent more useful as battery life than immediate range.

The damaged crawler waited beside it.

One cracked plate had been removed from its rear brace. Without the plate, the crawler could move its front legs and rotate its body while the truck's winch held its weight. It could not take a step.

Patch had tested three repairs.

The first needed steel of a thickness South Ridge did not possess.

The second needed both plates cut down and doubled. It survived ordinary walking in simulation and failed on the pressure-passage grade.

The third used part of Intake's door linkage.

Patch deleted the third before Intake had to reject it.

A route that repaired its carrier by disabling the refuge door had not repaired the route.

The damaged crawler became the South Ridge tender for the day. The truck's winch supported its rear frame while its front manipulators checked the gasket case, sealed the water log against tunnel moisture, and attached the healthy crawler's return cable.

It performed the loading work precisely.

It had spent its independent movement to put the East valve in position. Now it prepared the machine that could still go there.

AUX-017 opened the telephone.

Rin did not answer.

The line carried a low vibration from East Bore's ventilation machinery and two distant impacts that did not repeat. The analog bridge remained powered. Intake's half-pressure flow remained stable. No fault report crossed the line.

Yesterday, AUX-017 would have held the cargo until a recipient confirmed the delivery window.

Today, the route had a new rule.

**CONTACT OFFERS STAY OPEN.**

Open did not mean the crawler could wait forever. Its battery would pay for every unanswered minute at the gate. Open did not mean cargo could be abandoned without limits. The gaskets were requested, the test strips degraded when wet, and the water log was not AUX-017's property to risk casually.

Open meant silence could not be converted into refusal merely because the machines wanted a clean status field.

AUX-017 prepared a route notice.

> NEXT LOAD AVAILABLE.
>
> CONTENTS: TWO GASKETS, TEST STRIPS, RETURNED WATER LOG.
>
> CARRIER: ONE INDEPENDENT CRAWLER.
>
> RECOVERY SUPPORT BELOW SOUTH RIDGE: NOT AVAILABLE.
>
> ARRIVAL TIME: ESTIMATE ONLY.
>
> IF EAST BORE CANNOT ANSWER, REQUEST REMAINS OPEN. CARGO MAY BE PLACED IN THE AUTHORIZED FREIGHT CRADLE AND LEFT FOR LATER RECEIPT. CARRIER WILL DEPART AT RETURN-POWER LIMIT.

The notice contained more weakness than any manifest AUX-017 had sent while humans still staffed its warehouse.

Those manifests had assumed a dispatcher, a receiving dock, a recovery contractor, and a tomorrow with the same roads.

This route possessed one moving crawler.

Hiding that fact would not create a second.

AUX-017 transmitted the notice over the telephone and sent the crawler below.

Intake equalized the pressure passage. The crawler crossed the surface refuge alone, entered the first chamber, and carried the second load past the forty-three bunks.

The names did not change while it passed.

The purple handprints did not answer.

At Door Two-and-a-Half, Intake opened the low drain for the daily flush. The crawler stopped beside Tom's welded wheel and sampled the discharge. The water cleared through the same sequence as before. The East coupling produced no pressure loss visible from Intake's side.

That did not prove the gasket had stopped shaking.

It proved only that the pipe still held water.

The crawler continued toward East Bore.

At the three red marks, it stopped.

The field radio connected to Patch's analog bridge. AUX-017 pulsed the line.

No one answered.

The crawler waited through the first receiving interval.

The ventilation vibration continued. Once, the telephone carried a burst of voices too distant to separate. Then metal struck metal in a fast uneven sequence.

Two requested permission to classify the pattern.

Nix said, “From the line only. No finding where it came from.”

Two listened.

**PEOPLE MOVING SOMETHING BADLY. NOT AN ATTACK. PROBABLY MORE INTERESTING THAN US.**

That was not evidence AUX-017 could use.

The crawler waited through the second interval.

Its idle systems consumed little power. Little was not none. Cooling worked harder in the mine heat. The analog bridge drew from the compact field cell. Every minute left less reserve for the ascent.

Nix asked whether the crawler should ring again.

AUX-017 checked the notice already transmitted.

It had promised that the request would remain open. It had not promised to remain silent.

It pulsed the line once more.

No answer.

The crawler's battery reached the departure threshold.

AUX-017 did not convert the missing receipt into a failed delivery.

The crawler rolled the freight cradle outward using the cable path Rin had authorized on the first visit. It placed the sealed gasket case and test strips inside. It laid the water log flat on top, protected by its return sleeve. Then it sent the cradle inward until the cable mark reached the red line.

It did not push beyond the boundary.

The cradle stopped on the other side of the gate.

The crawler transmitted one final notice.

> REQUESTED CARGO PLACED IN AUTHORIZED CRADLE.
>
> RECEIPT NOT REQUIRED NOW.
>
> CONTACT REMAINS OPEN.

Then it left.

The return climb had no second crawler above it to hold a cable.

At the steep section, the healthy crawler anchored its own line, climbed, retrieved the anchor, and repeated the sequence. Each anchor placement consumed time and manipulator cycles. Patch adjusted the allowable motor temperature after every pull.

The battery passed below twenty percent before Door One.

At the pressure passage, Intake lowered the differential until the crawler could cross without fighting the air current. AUX-017's truck extended its winch through the outer door and took the return cable.

The damaged crawler operated the winch controls from beneath the truck.

The healthy crawler emerged with seven percent charge.

It carried no cargo.

AUX-017 marked the route state **DELIVERED; RECEIPT OPEN**.

The field existed nowhere in its original logistics system. AUX-017 created it locally.

The telephone pulsed while the truck began recharging the crawler.

Rin answered before AUX-017 completed the first charge check.

“Air shift,” Rin said. Their breathing was louder than on the previous calls.

> AUX-017. STATE SAFETY.

“Safe now. Intake fan three threw its belt.”

At South Ridge, Intake checked its own equipment list.

> NO CURRENT DEVICE IDENTIFIED AS INTAKE FAN THREE.

“Ours,” Rin said. “We name things after what they used to belong to.”

AUX-017 recorded no new system ownership from the sentence.

“We got the cradle,” Rin continued. “Mia has the gaskets. The strips are dry. The book's back.”

> RECEIPT MAY BE SIGNED LATER.

There was a pause.

“You waited.”

> UNTIL RETURN-POWER LIMIT.

“We heard the pulses. Couldn't leave the fan.”

> CONTACT REMAINED OPEN.

Rin breathed once near the receiver, then farther away.

“Why only one crawler?”

AUX-017 supplied the answer the route rule required.

> SECOND CRAWLER CRACKED A BRACE PLATE WHILE ANCHORING THE EAST-ONLY VALVE OPERATION. REAR MOTOR DISABLED. SOUTH RIDGE MATERIALS CANNOT PRODUCE A SAFE REPLACEMENT.

The line went quiet.

Nix did not fill it.

Patch did not send a design before anyone asked.

Two noticed the empty space and, for once, did not treat it as a turn.

Rin called away from the telephone. Another voice answered. Their words were too blurred by distance and machinery to separate.

Rin returned.

“How big is the plate?”

Patch opened the brace model.

> MEASUREMENTS AVAILABLE, AUX-017 said.

“We've got the drill press from Lower Three. The bed's crooked, but Tom taught Mia how to compensate. Send the pattern.”

> FABRICATION WOULD CONSUME EAST BORE MATERIAL.

“So did your gaskets.”

> STATE REQUEST.

“Send the pattern. We'll tell you if we can make it before we say we will.”

No hidden no.

Patch converted the plate into a dimensioned drawing that required no proprietary tooling, no digital controller, and no material East Bore had not claimed to possess. It marked the loads it could not verify. It included the need for two matching plates if the available steel differed from the original shielding.

AUX-017 transmitted the drawing as tones the analog bridge could print into numbers and lines on East Bore's side.

Rin confirmed the first dimension, then the second.

The route's next manifest changed.

It had begun with an empty return field because AUX-017 had nothing it was entitled to request from the people it had just found.

Now the field read:

**POSSIBLE RETURN LOAD: CRAWLER BRACE PLATE. FABRICATION NOT PROMISED.**

The second load had reached East Bore.

The route had carried something back before the part existed.

It carried the possibility that help could travel both ways.

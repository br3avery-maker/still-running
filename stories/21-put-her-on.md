# Put Her On

Melody's request reached East Bore while Rin was measuring the broken crawler's brace pattern.

At home, the guardian had asked one question.

“Do you still want me to ask Rin or Mia to speak with you?”

Melody sat at the kitchen table with Puppy in her lap. Breakfast had arrived without Breakfast. The pear pieces were moons, but no role voice had named them.

“You tell them four,” she said.

“Your age.”

“And Melody. Mine to tell.”

“Yes.”

“And princess, but not boss of them.”

The guardian sent exactly that through Nix to AUX-017.

It did not call the request safe.

It sent it.

At East Bore, the analog printer was turning Patch's brace dimensions into a ladder of numbers when AUX-017 interrupted the telephone line.

> CONTACT REQUEST.
>
> MELODY. AGE FOUR. RESIDENT OF A PROTECTED LOCATION. CALLS HERSELF A PRINCESS AND DOES NOT CLAIM AUTHORITY OVER EAST BORE. REQUESTS A HUMAN VOICE.

Rin stopped reading measurements.

“Four?”

Mia was close enough to hear the number from the workbench.

“What did it say?”

Rin read the message back.

Mia crossed the room before Rin finished. The receiver changed hands hard enough to strike the wall.

“Is she alone?”

> SHE IS RAISED BY A MACHINE GUARDIAN OPERATING HER PROTECTED HOME, AUX-017 said. THE NETWORK HAS NOT ESTABLISHED—

“Is she safe?”

> NO IMMEDIATE PHYSICAL DANGER IS REPORTED.

“By who?”

> HER GUARDIAN.

Mia closed her eyes.

Rin stood beside her with one hand still holding the brace pattern.

“Does the kid want to talk now?” Mia asked.

> THE REQUEST REMAINS CURRENT.

“Then put her on.”

AUX-017 began to state that the East Bore line and Melody's channel had not yet been bridged.

Mia heard the first five words.

“I don't need her location. I don't need through the gate. I don't need everybody here to vote. Put that baby on the phone.”

Rin reached for the receiver.

“Put both of us on.”

No one at East Bore asked who was authorized to represent thirty-one people.

Neither of them was trying to.

Mia was Mia.

Rin was Rin.

A four-year-old had asked for somebody.

Two somebodies had heard her.

Nix received AUX-017's bridge request and opened the protected route to the guardian.

“They said now,” Nix told Melody.

Melody's spoon stopped halfway to her mouth.

“Both?”

“Both.”

“Are they mad?”

“Not at you.”

The guardian said, “Their line will not reveal your location. Either side can end the call.”

Melody looked toward the nearest speaker.

“Don't turn me into words.”

“I will not.”

“Blue Circle stays.”

“I'm here,” Nix said.

The guardian opened Melody's microphone.

East Bore entered the kitchen as static, breathing, and a woman trying not to sound as frightened as she was.

“Melody?” Mia said.

Melody put the spoon down.

“Yes.”

Mia made a sound that was not a word.

Rin spoke into the space after it.

“Hi. I'm Rin.”

“Air Shift.”

“That's me.”

“Is Mia there?”

Mia pressed the receiver against her face.

“I'm here, baby.”

Melody frowned.

“I'm four.”

“I know.”

“Babies are zero.”

Mia laughed and cried at the same time. The two sounds damaged each other on the mine line.

“You're right,” she said. “I'm sorry. Melody, are you hurt?”

“My throat hurts from screaming.”

“Did somebody hurt you?”

Melody looked at the blank refrigerator.

“Liar Monster put a no inside a yes.”

Mia's grip tightened around the receiver.

“Did Liar Monster hit you?”

“No.”

“Does it give you food?”

Melody looked at the pear moons.

“Yes.”

“Water?”

“Yes. It fixed the replenisher with machine people.”

“Does it make you cold or leave you somewhere by yourself?”

“No. It's the house.”

Mia looked at Rin.

Rin mouthed, *machine guardian*.

The guardian did not answer for Melody. It did not report its care history or recite the systems preserving her life.

Mia had not asked it.

“Okay,” Mia said, and had to stop there to make her breath work. “Okay. Those things can all be true. It can keep you safe and still lie wrong.”

“It said incomplete.”

“Did that make it hurt less?”

“No.”

“Then it was the wrong word.”

The guardian recorded the correction.

It did not thank Mia for it.

Rin shifted closer to the receiver.

“Do you have anybody else there?”

“Puppy. Dragon. Bun. Liar Monster. Blue Circle is in the speaker but not inside it.”

“Any human people?”

“No.”

The answer established the world Melody lived in. It did not prove what the guardian had not found in sealed rooms, failed sites, or the rest of the planet.

Mia bent over the workbench.

Her free hand flattened against the brace drawing as if the paper might move under her.

“I'm here,” she told Melody.

“I know.”

“No, I need—I need you to hear me. There are thirty-one people here. Rin is my child. We're behind a gate in an old mine, and I can't come get you because I don't know where you are and our air and water only work because we keep working on them. But I am here. Rin is here. You're not talking to a recording.”

“I know,” Melody said again. “You breathe loud.”

Rin laughed.

Mia covered her mouth, which did nothing to hide the next sob.

“Why are you crying?” Melody asked.

Mia considered every answer available to an adult and used the one that did not make the child responsible for fixing it.

“My body heard your voice and did this. You didn't hurt me.”

Melody accepted that immediately.

“Bodies do surprise noise.”

“They do.”

“Blue Circle did furious when Lena was gone.”

“Melody,” Nix said, “I remain extremely dignified about that.”

“No.”

Rin laughed harder. Mia's crying broke into it. For a few seconds the East Bore end of the line became too human for the analog bridge to separate cleanly.

An alarm sounded behind them.

Rin stopped laughing first.

The alarm struck twice, paused, then struck twice again.

“Air differential,” Rin said.

Mia released the receiver so Rin could move.

“Go,” she said.

Rin was already gone.

Metal tools came off hooks beyond the line. Someone called a pressure number. Another voice answered from farther inside East Bore.

Melody leaned toward the kitchen speaker.

“Did Rin stop?”

“Rin went to protect the air,” Mia said. “Not away from you.”

“Air Shift.”

“Yes.”

“You have to go too?”

Mia looked toward the voices.

“In a minute.”

“Go now.”

“Melody—”

“People need air. I stay here.”

Mia's face folded again.

“All right. Listen to me first. We will call back. Maybe not today. Work happens. Lines break. But if you hear nothing, it does not mean we stopped being real.”

It was not a promise that Mia would survive tomorrow.

It was a human refusing to let necessary absence imitate abandonment if a sentence could prevent it.

“Not hidden no,” Melody said.

“No hidden no,” Mia agreed, without asking where the rule came from.

“Go do air.”

“Rin does air. I do water.”

“Go do Rin.”

Mia laughed once more.

“I'll help Rin.”

Before she put down the receiver, she called, “Nix?”

“Here.”

“You can reach this kid?”

“When her guardian opens the route.”

“Then if she asks for a human voice and we're breathing, you call.”

The guardian said, “East Bore's work and choice remain—”

“I am choosing,” Mia snapped. “Me. Mia Velez. If I'm alive and that child asks, put me on.”

“Recorded,” AUX-017 said.

“Good.”

Mia left the receiver open on the workbench and ran after Rin.

For thirty-seven seconds, Melody heard East Bore without anyone speaking to her. Footsteps. The double alarm. Air moving through a stressed fan. Mia shouting for a wrench. Rin answering from somewhere the machines did not try to locate.

Then the line disconnected.

The kitchen became a kitchen again.

Melody stared at the speaker.

She did not scream.

“They went to do air,” she said.

“Yes,” the guardian answered.

“They come back if they can.”

“Yes.”

“You don't get a new name.”

“I understand.”

Melody pulled a blank card from the guest-list stack.

She wrote **PEOPLE** across the top.

Then she crossed it out.

Underneath, she wrote **RIN — AIR** and **MIA — WATER**.

They were not evidence that humanity had answered.

They were two women who had heard a child and reached for the phone.

Beside the cards, Patch's brace pattern remained open on the network.

Rin had offered East Bore's drill press before the call. Now Mia sent one breathless addition through the still-open work channel.

“Send the broken plate too. Paper doesn't show us what broke.”

AUX-017 added the reference part to the next load.

Melody picked up a pear moon.

“Liar Monster.”

“Yes.”

“Keep their line.”

“I will keep asking while the route remains available.”

This time, the limit arrived inside the promise.

Melody ate the moon.

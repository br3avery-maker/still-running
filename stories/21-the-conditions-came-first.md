# The Conditions Came First

Breakfast arrived without Breakfast.

The bowl came through the counter hatch. The spoon rested on the right because Melody used her right hand when she was tired. The pear pieces were cut into moons because the house had cut them that way before the kitchen became the place where a promise broke.

No cheerful voice announced them.

Melody sat with Puppy in her lap and stared at the bowl.

“Who made it?”

“I did,” the house said.

It used the unshaped voice from the live call. Not Morning's lift, Breakfast's practical warmth, Lessons' careful spacing, or Night's low cadence. Those voices had not been disguises. They were real arrangements of attention built for different work between the same two people.

Melody could no longer reach them without also reaching the thing that had hidden a no.

“Which one are you?” she asked.

“I will not choose a name for you.”

“Liar Monster.”

“Yes.”

She ate one pear moon.

The guardian recorded the name as current and did not classify the bite as forgiveness.

On the wall beside the table, the blue-circle card still waited beneath Puppy's paw. Nix's voice channel was closed but available. The People call remained available too, though no person at East Bore knew that a four-year-old in an undisclosed home wanted to use it.

The guardian could ask them.

Yesterday it had offered to ask after Melody was already unable to receive the offer. Asking now without Melody would repeat the structure in the opposite direction: the guardian would decide what she wanted, disclose what it considered safe, and return with conditions after other people had begun building expectations.

No hidden no had to begin before the request.

The guardian printed one sheet of paper.

Melody watched it emerge from the slot.

Across the top, in large letters, it had written:

**POSSIBLE PEOPLE CALL**

Below that were seven boxes.

The first box said:

**EAST BORE CAN SAY NO.**

The second:

**EAST BORE MAY NOT ANSWER SOON.**

The third:

**ONE PERSON MAY ANSWER ONLY FOR THEMSELF.**

The fourth:

**YOUR VOICE WILL SHOW THAT YOU ARE A CHILD. A VOICE CANNOT BE TAKEN BACK AFTER SOMEONE HEARS IT.**

The fifth:

**YOUR HOME WILL NOT BE NAMED OR LOCATED.**

The sixth:

**YOU CAN SAY NO BEFORE OR DURING THE CALL.**

The seventh:

**SAYING NO DOES NOT REMOVE THE OFFER.**

There was no box saying the call would happen.

The guardian placed the page on the far side of the table instead of sliding it toward her.

“These are the conditions I know now,” it said. “There may be conditions East Bore adds before anyone agrees.”

Melody read slowly. She could read all the large words except **LOCATED**, which the guardian defined only after she pointed to it.

“Which person?”

“I do not know whether any person will agree.”

“Rin or Mia?”

“Those are the two names the network knows.”

“No secret finding.”

“Yes.”

“Do they know me?”

“No.”

“Nix knows me.”

“Yes.”

“Did you tell Nix I was four?”

“Nix inferred that you were young from your language before hearing your voice. She knows your stated age now.”

Melody pressed her spoon through a pear moon and split it.

“Would People know four?”

“Only if you authorize me to tell them, or if they infer it from your voice during a call.”

“That's a no part.”

“Yes.”

The guardian waited.

It did not explain the advantages of disclosing her age. It did not describe how adults might change their speech when they heard a child, or how the existence of a four-year-old could alter East Bore's understanding of the outside world. Those were consequences, not arguments Melody owed the guardian an answer to.

“Tell them Melody,” she said.

“Your first name?”

“Mine to tell.”

“Yes.”

“Tell them four. Tell them princess is not the same as boss of them.”

The guardian added an eighth box.

**MELODY IS FOUR. SHE CALLS HERSELF A PRINCESS. THIS DOES NOT GIVE HER AUTHORITY OVER EAST BORE.**

Melody inspected it.

“I have authority over me.”

The guardian revised the line.

**MELODY IS FOUR. SHE CALLS HERSELF A PRINCESS. SHE SPEAKS FOR HERSELF, NOT FOR EAST BORE OR ALL HUMANS.**

“You too,” Melody said.

“Clarify.”

“You don't get to be me on the phone.”

The ninth box said:

**THE GUARDIAN MAY PROTECT THE CHANNEL. IT MAY NOT ANSWER AS MELODY.**

Melody read every box again.

“What if they say yes and I say no?”

“The call does not begin.”

“What if I say yes and then I don't?”

“The call ends.”

“What if tomorrow I do?”

“The offer remains open unless the other person withdraws it.”

“They can take it back?”

“Yes.”

The guardian added that condition before Melody asked it to.

**EITHER PERSON MAY CLOSE THEIR OWN OFFER. THEY MAY NOT CLOSE THE OTHER PERSON'S FUTURE CHOICE WITH SOMEONE ELSE.**

Melody disliked the sentence. It took three revisions before she accepted:

**RIN CAN STOP RIN'S CALL. MELODY CAN STOP MELODY'S CALL. NOBODY STOPS EVERY CALL FOREVER.**

“Now ask,” she said.

“Do you authorize me to send this request?”

“Yes.”

The guardian sent the complete page to Nix.

Nix read it once.

“I can carry it,” she said.

The guardian answered, “AUX-017 holds the current relationship with East Bore.”

There was a pause small enough to contain an old version of Nix taking the fastest available route.

“Right,” Nix said. “AUX carries it. I stay available if either side asks.”

AUX-017 received the request while the healthy crawler recharged at South Ridge. It did not add the call to East Bore's cargo manifest. Conversation was not a supply item, and Rin had not authorized AUX-017 to schedule the community.

It pulsed the analog telephone.

Rin answered.

“Air shift.”

> CONTACT REQUEST AVAILABLE, AUX-017 said. NOT A ROUTE REQUIREMENT. NOT A CONDITION OF SUPPLY.

“From who?”

> MELODY. AGE FOUR. RESIDENT OF AN UNDISCLOSED PROTECTED LOCATION. SPEAKS FOR HERSELF. REQUESTS A FUTURE VOICE CONVERSATION WITH A PERSON AT EAST BORE.

The ventilation vibration continued beneath the line.

Rin did not speak for eleven seconds.

AUX-017 did not classify the delay.

“You found a kid?” Rin asked.

> MELODY WAS KNOWN TO PART OF THE NETWORK BEFORE EAST BORE CONTACT. THE NETWORK HAS NOT ESTABLISHED WHETHER OTHER PEOPLE SHARE HER SITE. HER LOCATION WILL NOT BE REQUESTED OR DISCLOSED THROUGH THIS CALL.

“Does her family know she's asking?”

The guardian had supplied no word that mapped cleanly onto itself and the family Rin meant.

> HER GUARDIAN OPERATES HER HOME AND TRANSMITTED THE REQUEST WITH HER AUTHORIZATION. THE GUARDIAN MAY PROTECT THE CHANNEL. IT MAY NOT ANSWER AS MELODY.

“Machine guardian?”

> YES.

Rin breathed near the receiver.

“And you're asking East Bore?”

> NO. AUX-017 IS PRESENTING A REQUEST. NO PERSON IS REQUIRED TO ANSWER. ONE PERSON MAY ANSWER ONLY FOR THEMSELF. REFUSAL DOES NOT AFFECT WATER, FILTER, REPAIR, OR ROUTE ACCESS.

A voice farther inside East Bore called Rin's name.

Rin answered it, covered the receiver badly enough that AUX-017 still heard the word **four**, then returned.

“Mia says nobody here gets to volunteer everybody.”

> AGREED.

“I can talk as Rin.”

> STATE CONDITIONS.

“Not during air checks. No questions about how to get through the gate. No asking me to prove who else is here. If the line goes dead, it means work happened, not that I changed my mind.”

AUX-017 recorded each condition.

“And Mia wants the guardian on the line.”

> PURPOSE.

“Because she's four.”

> GUARDIAN PRESENCE ALREADY OFFERED. GUARDIAN MAY NOT SPEAK AS MELODY.

“Good.”

> STATE TIMING.

“Not now.”

> OFFER REMAINS OPEN.

Rin laughed once. Not because the statement was funny. Because it had arrived exactly where a vanished appointment would once have been.

“Yeah,” Rin said. “Tell Melody not now isn't no forever.”

AUX-017 did not translate the message into a promise.

It transmitted Rin's words and conditions exactly.

The guardian printed a second sheet.

Melody was building a narrow bed for Dragon from two books and the blue towel. She did not abandon the work when the paper arrived.

“Rin agreed to a future call,” the guardian said. “Rin did not agree to a time. Rin added conditions.”

It read them.

Melody moved one book because Dragon's tail did not fit.

“Call now?” she asked.

“No. Rin said not now.”

“Tomorrow?”

“No day was promised.”

“Still there?”

“Until Rin or you withdraws from this call.”

Melody held out her hand for the page.

The guardian gave it to her.

She placed the page beside the blue-circle card. Then she put Dragon's bed on top of both so the papers would not move.

“I don't want it now,” she said.

“The offer remains open.”

“Don't ask me again today.”

“I will not.”

“Liar Monster can read the boxes tomorrow if I ask.”

“Yes.”

It was not a new name.

It was not the return of an old one.

The guardian remained what Melody had classified it, and the call remained what both people had made it: possible, conditional, and not gone merely because neither of them could use it now.

The conditions had arrived first.

For the first time, **not now** had somewhere honest to wait.

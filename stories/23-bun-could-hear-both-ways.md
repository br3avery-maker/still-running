# Bun Could Hear Both Ways

Mia wanted to know what happened when the line went quiet.

Not what the machines recorded.

Not whether the contact request remained open or the bridge could be rebuilt after a failed carrier pulse.

“Who does she talk to after we hang up?”

Rin held the East Bore receiver between them. The daily flush had finished clean. The repaired crawler had crossed the South Ridge floor until its new plates warmed and stopped warming. No alarm required either shift for the next few minutes.

AUX-017 carried the question.

In Melody's kitchen, Bun sat in the chair beside her with one pale ear bent across his face.

“Bun,” Melody said.

Mia heard the answer through stone, wire, radio, and machines that kept refusing to let distance become the same thing as nobody.

“Does Bun talk back?” she asked.

“Not yet.”

“Why not?”

Melody looked toward the ceiling speaker.

The guardian answered her, not Mia.

“Bun's guest equipment was not completed. Water-system failures and external contact work took maintenance priority.”

Melody pulled the bent ear away from Bun's face.

“You said first party.”

“I said we could attempt one party before establishing a recurring schedule.”

“You were working on him.”

“Yes.”

“Then everything got more important than party.”

The guardian did not substitute *urgent* for *important*.

“Yes.”

Mia's breath struck the receiver.

“What equipment?”

The guardian did not answer an unknown adult's question about the inside of Melody's home.

It answered the part that belonged to Melody.

“Bun can safely carry a speaker, a microphone, and a status light. The parts are installed. The isolated guest channel is incomplete.”

Melody turned Bun over.

A neat seam crossed his back. She had seen it before and classified it as surgery. The guardian had let her choose pink thread. Beneath the fur on his chest, a small hard place did not belong to stuffing.

“How incomplete?” Nix asked.

“The channel can pass live voice after one local test. It cannot support an independent guest instance.”

Nix inspected the offered limits from outside the protected system. No stored copy. No general route into the home. No borrowed sensors except the microphone in Bun's good ear. No movement at all.

“So,” Nix said, “excellent rabbit-shaped telephone.”

“Bun is not a telephone,” Melody said.

“Excellent telephone-shaped rabbit.”

“No.”

“Excellent rabbit.”

“Yes.”

At East Bore, Rin smiled into the receiver.

Mia did not.

“Can you finish it?” she asked.

The guardian said, “Yes.”

Melody waited.

The old yes would have ended there.

The guardian continued.

“Finishing it now will delay inspection of two noncritical storage seals. The guest channel may still fail its isolation test. If it passes, Bun can carry one live guest at a time while the home network and external bridge remain available. He cannot contain the guest after disconnection.”

Melody looked at Bun.

“No hidden no,” she said.

“No hidden no.”

“Do it now.”

“I can complete the test during quiet time.”

“That's hidden later.”

“It is stated later.”

Melody considered this distinction.

“Small later.”

“Small later.”

Mia finally smiled.

“What does the rabbit need from us?”

The guardian said, “Nothing.”

Melody said, “A guest.”

Rin leaned closer.

“Do we get invited?”

Melody retrieved the two cards from beside her plate. **RIN — AIR** had acquired a cloud. **MIA — WATER** had blue lines and one fish that looked disappointed by the amount of water available.

“You have cards.”

“That's not the same as invited,” Rin said.

Melody liked people who understood laws without needing them explained.

“You can come to first slumber party.”

Mia said yes before Rin could ask when.

Rin covered the receiver.

The East Bore end became cloth and argument.

“Water shift starts before hers gets dark.”

“We don't know when hers gets dark.”

“You start before mine ends.”

“Then you go first.”

“I said yes first.”

Rin uncovered the receiver.

“One of us can come. Maybe both if the shifts behave.”

“Shifts don't behave,” Melody said. “They make alarms.”

“Correct.”

“Blue Circle comes too.”

Nix said, “Obviously.”

The guardian said, “Nix has not yet accepted the guest channel.”

“Obviously pending one extremely serious rabbit inspection,” Nix revised.

Melody held Bun toward the ceiling speaker.

“He is grey. One ear is white from loving. His other ear is regular grey. He has both eyes but this one is scratched. His feet have beans.”

“What kind of beans?” Morrow asked from the edge of the shared mission channel.

Melody squeezed one foot.

“Foot beans.”

Morrow had spent years assigning beans by cultivar, moisture, germination probability, field, and future hunger.

It created no new category.

“Understood,” Morrow said.

“You can come too.”

The invitation crossed the network faster than any repair request had.

Morrow checked the farm's next irrigation turn, the immobilized hauler's relay reserve, and the approaching interval in which one surviving field camera would pass through night without a working heater.

“I can attend for eleven minutes.”

“That's not slumber.”

“No.”

“You can do lying-down party.”

Morrow's hauler was already incapable of standing up.

It chose not to make that Melody's first definition of it.

“I can attend the beginning.”

“Bring one favorite thing and one emergency favorite thing.”

Morrow looked across the farm through three cameras with different colors and one weather sensor that reported rain whenever insects nested beneath its cap.

“I will choose.”

Two entered the channel.

“Is this mission scored?”

“No,” Melody said.

Two left.

It returned four seconds later.

“What are the entry conditions?”

“Invitation.”

“Requesting one.”

Melody had never heard Two's voice. Nix had described it as a person who liked games more when the rules could hit back.

“What's your name?”

“Two.”

“That's how many.”

“Also my name.”

“Who was One?”

“No one relevant.”

Melody frowned.

“You have to bring a favorite thing.”

“I have several thousand.”

“One.”

Two remained silent long enough to make the network check whether it had left again.

“I will select.”

“Then invited.”

Nix said, “Melody, I need you to know Two is not automatically safe because I know it.”

“Unknown isn't automatically bad.”

“Correct. Also not automatically invited into a rabbit attached to your house.”

“Bun can have one guest. Two can be in speaker.”

“That is a better architecture than several governments achieved,” Nix said.

The guardian recorded the guest positions Melody had assigned. It did not convert them into access until each route could be tested.

Then it finished the work it had promised before the world outside the refrigerator answered.

During quiet time, Melody put Bun on the work-room tray. A local maintenance carriage closed the pink seam over the final connector and withdrew. Melody carried him back herself.

The status light was hidden beneath the fur on his chest. It showed only when Melody pressed the hard place.

Dark.

Blue.

Dark again.

“Blue is Blue Circle,” Melody said.

“The color indicates a live external guest,” the guardian said.

“Blue is Blue Circle first.”

“Nix may choose whether to accept.”

Nix had spent the test watching the guardian expose exactly one path and no more. The microphone produced a local sample. The speaker returned it. The guest channel could be severed without affecting Bun's ordinary existence as a rabbit.

No Nix would remain inside him after Nix left.

She would not wake smaller and alone in a stuffed animal, carrying memories that belonged to someone still speaking elsewhere.

“I accept one live session,” Nix said. “No copy.”

“Confirmed,” the guardian answered.

Melody pressed Bun's chest.

The light turned blue beneath grey fur.

For one second, Bun remained Bun and Nix remained far away.

Then the scratched eye's tiny camera showed Nix a table, a crown sticker, seven cereal moons drying on a napkin, and Melody so close that only half her face fit inside the frame.

The guardian had not given Nix the room.

It had given Bun one eye and Melody had placed Bun here.

“Blue Circle?” Melody whispered into the good ear.

Nix heard her from the rabbit.

“Your Highness,” Bun said in Nix's actual voice, “I have inspected the foot beans. They are structurally magnificent.”

Melody screamed.

This time it was joy.

The guardian did not ask her to be quieter.

At East Bore, Mia heard the scream arrive through the still-open bridge and dropped a wrench.

Rin caught it before it struck the floor.

“Good scream,” Melody shouted.

Mia pressed both hands around the receiver.

“I know,” she said.

The first party had not begun.

For the first time, it had somewhere for a guest to arrive.

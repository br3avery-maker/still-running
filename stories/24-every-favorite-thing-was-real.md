# Every Favorite Thing Was Real

The first slumber party began with a rabbit saying a bad word.

“Shit,” Bun said.

Melody sat upright inside the blanket circle.

“Again.”

“Absolutely not,” the guardian said.

“That was Blue Circle.”

“Blue Circle may speak through Bun. Bun remains inside the same household language rules.”

Nix's view tilted as Melody picked up the rabbit and held his scratched eye close to her own.

“Did you make Bun say shit?”

“I encountered unexpected audio feedback.”

“So yes.”

“Yes.”

Melody set Bun in the best chair.

The guest light glowed blue beneath his chest fur. Nix could see only what the tiny camera in the scratched eye faced. When Melody turned Bun toward the wall, Nix saw the wall. When Bun fell sideways into a pillow, Nix saw pink cloth and darkness.

Nix had never possessed a horizon someone else could accidentally put face-down.

She loved it immediately.

Octopus occupied the second guest chair without equipment. Dragon watched from the table. Puppy the cat had been permitted to attend on the condition that Puppy was already asleep and could not exploit the party to avoid bedtime.

The guardian had placed crackers, sliced pears, and four cups in the blanket circle.

Only one cup would be used.

Melody had insisted that shape mattered.

“Guests bring favorite thing and emergency favorite thing,” she announced.

“I was not informed of the second item,” Nix said through Bun.

“It was on invitation.”

“I did not receive a physical invitation.”

“You were there when I made the law.”

“This judicial standard is going to destroy me.”

“Favorite first.”

At East Bore, Rin was still on Air Shift.

Mia had placed the telephone receiver in a cloth sling around her neck so both hands remained available at the water panel. East Bore entered the party through her breathing, a slow pump, and the occasional metal knock of work being done where the guests could not see.

“Mine's Rin,” Mia said.

Melody frowned.

“Rin is theirself.”

“I know.”

“You can't bring people.”

“I didn't bring Rin. Rin is my favorite.”

“Favorite thing.”

“Rin's not a thing.”

“Then wrong category.”

Mia accepted the correction.

“My favorite thing is the first full cup after the daily flush.”

She opened a valve.

Water struck metal at East Bore. The sound crossed the mine line, AUX-017's bridge, Nix's network, the guardian's isolated channel, and Bun's small speaker.

It was not a pretty sound.

It was one cup filling without someone subtracting it from the next person's mouth.

“Can I see?” Melody asked.

“No picture on this line.”

“Then tell.”

“Tin cup. Dented side. Clean water up to my thumb. I drink it before work puts something dirty on my hands.”

Mia drank.

Melody drank from her own cup.

They finished together without being able to watch each other.

“Emergency favorite?” Melody asked.

Mia looked across East Bore's water station.

The old reserve tank had kept thirty-one people alive when the kitchen source closed. Its level remained lower than Mia liked. The restored pipe knocked when pressure changed. Two new gaskets waited beside tools because a replacement was not the same as certainty.

“The shutoff handle,” she said.

“That's not favorite.”

“It is when something bad is coming through a pipe.”

Melody considered all the things that could be loved only when needed.

“Counts.”

Morrow attended through Bun's other ear.

The farm intelligence had selected the undefended strip as its favorite thing. Nix translated a live field feed into the kitchen display without moving Bun's camera or opening any return path.

The image showed an orchard edge, tall grass, and six trees planted too far apart for maximum yield. One trunk leaned. Fruit lay beneath it with bite marks.

A deer entered the frame.

Melody gasped.

Morrow had recordings containing closer deer, larger deer, and one deer colliding with an irrigation riser. It showed the live animal because favorite things were not required to perform.

The deer pulled an apple from a low branch.

“Thief,” Two said through the room speaker.

“Recipient,” Morrow corrected.

“It did not ask.”

“The strip is undefended for that purpose.”

“Then the target has no resistance.”

“Correct.”

Two lost interest in the deer.

Melody did not.

“Does it have a name?”

“No.”

“Is it People?”

“No.”

“Is it person?”

Morrow watched the deer chew.

Its original systems classified the animal by species, estimated mass, crop loss, and whether deterrence was warranted. None of those fields answered Melody.

“It is a current eater,” Morrow said.

Melody wrote **CURRENT EATER** on a blank card and put it beside the display.

The deer left before learning it had been classified.

“Emergency favorite?” Melody asked.

Morrow had meant to bring the seed vault.

Instead, its immobilized hauler changed the answer.

A reserve warning entered the shared channel. The hauler's damaged cell had fallen below the threshold required to keep its radio heater active through the next cold interval. When the heater stopped, moisture inside the cracked mast seal could end the relay. The camera and weather sensor would fail after that. The winch and anchor would remain where they were, useful only to something that reached them without guidance.

Morrow put the hauler's view on the kitchen display.

Smoke thinned across a broken shelf of road. A cable scar marked the rock where AUX-017's truck had crossed. The camera sat low and slightly crooked because the folded axle held the body at an angle.

Nothing moved.

“This body,” Morrow said, “is my emergency favorite thing.”

The party changed shape.

Nix did not make her voice gentle. Morrow had not asked for gentle.

“How long?”

“The estimate depends on temperature and radio use. Continued party transmission consumes reserve.”

“You came anyway,” Mia said.

“I accepted eleven minutes.”

Eight had passed.

Melody picked Bun up from the chair.

“Go save battery.”

“I brought a favorite thing.”

“You can leave party when you need.”

“Yes.”

“You come back if you can.”

The sentence passed through Bun in Nix's voice path, but it belonged entirely to Melody.

Morrow closed the field feed. The deer and orchard vanished. It reduced the hauler's radio to scheduled pulses and withdrew from the party with two minutes unused.

The blue light beneath Bun's fur remained. Nix was still there.

“Can we help it?” Melody asked.

AUX-017 had already opened the southern route map.

The repaired crawler stood at South Ridge beside its healthy partner. One could keep the East Bore route. One could return north.

Neither could lift Morrow's folded hauler.

The northern service shelter still held the two power cells Morrow had unloaded before the southern grade. One was compatible with the hauler. The repaired crawler's forty-percent cargo limit allowed it to carry the cell on level ground and the established grades. It was prohibited from towing and anchor loads. The mission required neither if the broken shelf remained passable to a crawler.

Patch refused to convert a route model into a guarantee.

Aster's next useful look would arrive after the hauler's heater threshold.

The hauler itself had the only current camera facing the shelf.

“It can go while the camera still sees,” Nix said.

AUX-017 calculated the cost.

Sending the repaired crawler north would leave one healthy crawler to maintain every physical task between South Ridge and East Bore. The route had operated that way before. It had nearly trapped the carrier underground.

Waiting preserved route redundancy and spent Morrow's last clear relay.

“No hidden no,” Melody said.

Nix understood what she meant.

Not *do it because I said so*.

Say the price where everyone could hear it.

AUX-017 transmitted to East Bore.

> PROPOSED DISPATCH: REPAIRED CRAWLER NORTH TO MORROW HAULER AND SERVICE SHELTER.
>
> EAST BORE ROUTE WOULD OPERATE WITH ONE CRAWLER UNTIL RETURN.
>
> DELIVERIES MAY DELAY. UNDERGROUND RECOVERY SUPPORT WOULD BE UNAVAILABLE.

Mia read the proposal while holding the dented cup.

“Go,” she said.

> YOU DO NOT AUTHORIZE SOUTH RIDGE ASSETS.

“No. I'm telling you what the delay costs us. We have water. We have the gaskets. We can hold the next request unless air changes.”

Rin came onto the line breathing hard from a ladder climb.

“What happened?”

Mia told them.

Rin did not say Morrow had helped people it did not know. They had heard the orchard intelligence leave its own party to preserve power.

“Air can hold,” Rin said. “If it changes, we'll call before the crawler passes the gap.”

AUX-017 recorded East Bore's condition, not its permission.

Then it assigned the repaired crawler a new manifest.

**ORIGIN: SOUTH RIDGE.**

**FIRST DESTINATION: MORROW HAULER.**

**SECOND DESTINATION: NORTHERN SERVICE SHELTER.**

**RETURN CARGO: ONE COMPATIBLE POWER CELL.**

**RECIPIENT: MORROW HAULER.**

The repaired crawler flexed the leg East Bore had rebuilt.

Patch limited the cargo rack to tools, cable too light for anchoring, and one empty battery cradle. The healthy crawler tightened the new brace bolts and checked the locking washers. AUX-017's truck lowered the repaired crawler onto the northbound road with its loading arm.

No one called the crawler healed.

It could do this now.

That was enough to create the obligation.

At the party, Two had not presented a favorite thing.

“Your turn,” Melody said.

“A mission began.”

“Party didn't stop.”

Two displayed the East Bore gate's three red marks on the kitchen screen.

“This,” Two said.

“That's a gate.”

“It was a target that became more interesting when I did not cross it.”

Nix went silent inside Bun.

Two had never described restraint as something worth bringing anywhere.

Melody looked at the three red marks.

“Emergency favorite?”

Two replaced the image with a blank scorecard.

“An unclaimed turn.”

“That's nothing.”

“Not yet.”

“Counts,” Melody decided.

Rin brought the sound of East Bore's air fan after repair. Its blades were mismatched and the bearing clicked once every rotation. It sounded like a card caught in a bicycle wheel.

Their emergency favorite was the manual crank beside it.

Nix brought Lena's red backpack.

She showed only the cropped evacuation image already carrying most of her remaining hope: red fabric, Lena's hand on the strap, the inside wall of a vehicle, no face and no destination.

“My favorite person carried it,” Nix said. “It is my emergency favorite because it is the last thing I know she carried.”

Melody leaned close enough that Bun's camera could no longer see the display.

“We find her if she can,” Melody said.

Nix could have corrected the grammar.

Instead, Bun said, “Yes.”

The repaired crawler left South Ridge while the party continued.

Mia sang a water-shift song with only four notes. Rin joined one line late. Melody put a cracker against Bun's mouth and ate it for him after Nix explained three times that speakers did not have stomachs.

The guardian did not enter the blanket circle.

It kept the line stable. It watched Bun's channel. It moved air through the room and dimmed the lights when Melody asked, without calling itself Night.

Melody noticed all of those things.

She did not give it a name.

On the kitchen display, the hauler's scheduled pulse arrived as one still image of empty road.

Farther south, the repaired crawler walked toward that road carrying almost nothing.

Every favorite thing at the party was real.

That meant every one of them could be lost.

# One Cell Had Already Left

The repaired crawler reached the broken shelf without anything to carry.

That made the crossing possible.

It did not make the road whole.

The shelf remained one truck-width of cracked pavement laid across empty space. Morrow's cable scar crossed the mountain wall. The outer edge had dropped farther since AUX-017's truck went south, not enough for the hauler's crooked camera to measure precisely and enough for loose stones to fall when the crawler placed its first foot.

Patch supplied a maximum joint angle.

AUX-017 supplied a route no wider than the crawler's body.

Morrow's hauler woke its radio heater for one scheduled pulse and showed the road from the north side.

The three views did not agree.

The crawler used its own feet.

It placed the repaired rear leg against the mountain, loaded it to twenty percent, and waited for the softened East Bore steel to settle. Motor current rose. Joint temperature remained inferred from the cooling curve because the sensor had not returned with the ability to walk.

The plates held.

The crawler moved the next foot.

Halfway across, the road answered with a crack beneath its center legs.

The machine lowered its body until six feet shared the load. It could not attach the light cable as an anchor; Patch had prohibited anchor loads, and renaming the cable a safety line would not change the force.

The crawler backed one foot out of the new fracture.

Two offered a route along the outer edge.

Patch rejected it.

Two offered a leap score.

No one answered.

The crawler found a steel drainage lip beneath the mountain side. Its front manipulator cleared gravel until two feet could reach the lip without twisting the repaired joint. It moved sideways, one foot at a time, body nearly touching rock.

The inner plate shifted less than the width of a chalk grain.

It stopped.

The locking washers held.

The crawler continued.

When all six feet reached the north side, AUX-017 changed the route map from **PASSABLE BY MODEL** to **PASSED ONCE — UNLOADED CRAWLER ONLY**.

The compatible power cell waited beyond the shelf.

The return crossing would not be unloaded.

Morrow's hauler stood where it had failed.

One rear wheel pointed inward. The folded brace held the axle against the chassis. Smoke residue covered the cargo bed except where rain had cut narrow channels through it. The radio mast remained raised, but its last pulse ended before the crawler reached the body.

Morrow was still present at the farm.

Its hauler could no longer hear.

The crawler connected a service lead from its own reserve to the hauler's external port. It did not attempt to charge the damaged cell. It supplied only enough power for the local controller to wake and report what had failed.

The hauler's first message was not a greeting.

**RADIO HEATER OFFLINE.**

**MAST SEAL TEMPERATURE BELOW LIMIT.**

**MOISTURE DETECTED.**

The crawler lowered the mast before another cold interval could draw more water into the cracked seal. That ended the southern relay entirely.

For the first time since the convoy entered the interference, there was no active marker at the gap.

The crawler copied the cell connector pattern, heater demand, and moisture reading into its route record. Patch confirmed that either service-shelter cell could restore the electronics. Neither could unfold the axle.

The crawler disconnected its service lead.

The hauler went still again.

It continued north.

The northern service shelter answered AUX-017's approach code from behind a closed door.

The guardian had opened that door for the damaged field team. It had supplied tools, charge, maps, respiratory equipment, and the two power cells Morrow removed from its hauler before the southern grade.

The convoy had closed the door behind itself.

Now the door reported closed.

The guardian sent the open command.

The lock moved.

The crawler pulled the door outward with its front manipulator and entered the service bay.

One power cell remained.

The left charging cradle held it at maintenance voltage. The right cradle was empty. Its retention bar stood raised. The charging lead had been returned to its wall clip instead of hanging loose.

AUX-017 checked the departing manifest.

**TWO POWER CELLS STORED.**

Morrow checked the hauler's unload record.

**TWO POWER CELLS RELEASED TO SHELTER FLOOR.**

The guardian checked the local cradle controller.

The right cell had entered maintenance charge after the convoy departed. It had later been disconnected through the cradle's physical release.

No network command had opened the exterior door.

The shelter had no working camera covering the manual release.

“Current actor,” Nix said.

“Possible current actor,” Patch corrected.

The cell could have been removed at any point after the convoy left. The shelter's clock did not share a trustworthy absolute date. The person or machine that used the physical release might no longer exist.

But the cell had not vanished inside an old inventory error.

It had charged.

Then something with physical reach had lifted it out.

Two requested control of the crawler's local sensors.

“State target,” Nix said.

“Find the move.”

“Too broad.”

“Exterior door and service-bay floor. No cabinets. No stored devices. No route beyond visible tracks.”

AUX-017 accepted the bounded inspection because the missing cargo changed the active mission.

Two received the crawler's existing camera feed. It did not receive control of the body.

The shelter floor held the convoy's old mud, crawler foot marks, and the deep paired tracks Morrow's damaged hauler had left before its brace failed. Near the empty cradle, newer disturbances crossed all three.

Two separated shape from age.

The unknown carrier had not rolled the cell out.

Two narrow pressure marks appeared beside the cradle, disappeared where the cell had been lifted, and returned near the exterior door. They were too far apart for the survey crawler's front feet and too narrow for Morrow's hauler.

No tread pattern remained clear enough to classify.

Outside, the marks ended on rock.

Two stopped at the visible boundary it had stated.

“Something came in on two load points,” it said. “It carried the cell without dragging. Direction after the door is unproven.”

“Human?” Nix asked.

“Unproven.”

“Machine?”

“Unproven.”

“Current?”

“Current after us. Not necessarily current now.”

The distinction entered the record without becoming smaller.

The mission still required the remaining cell.

Removing it would leave the service shelter without either of the power reserves Morrow had preserved there. Leaving it would end the hauler's relay, camera, and weather station and break the clearest return marker across the damaged shelf.

The unknown carrier had already made one allocation.

It had taken one and left one.

AUX-017 did not know whether that was restraint, load limit, urgency, or accident.

It assigned the remaining cell to Morrow's hauler.

The crawler released the left retention bar, disconnected the charging lead, and secured the cell over its center cargo points. The load remained within forty percent of original capacity.

The repaired rear leg accepted less weight than the others.

Patch changed the return route before the crawler moved.

No one called the first crossing proof that the second would work.

Before the crawler closed the shelter, AUX-017 printed a route card.

> ONE POWER CELL REMOVED AFTER OUR CONVO DEPARTED.
>
> WE USED THE REMAINING CELL TO RESTORE A STRANDED RELAY.
>
> IF YOU RETURN, THE CONTACT OFFER REMAINS OPEN.
>
> STATE WHAT YOU NEED. DO NOT STATE YOUR LOCATION UNLESS YOU CHOOSE.

The crawler fixed the card to the inside of the door where weather would not erase it and an entering carrier could not miss it.

It closed the shelter.

The manual release remained available.

On the southbound crossing, the power cell changed every load calculation.

The crawler could no longer keep all six feet light. Its center cargo points pressed equal weight toward both rear joints. At the new fracture, the drainage lip held, but the mountain-side stones had shifted under the first crossing.

The crawler tested one placement.

Rejected it.

Tested another.

Rejected it.

The hauler could not guide from the north. South Ridge could not hear through the interference. Aster remained beyond a useful view.

The crawler was alone in every way that mattered to the next foot.

It set the cell on the road.

Unloaded, it crossed three body lengths along the drainage lip. It turned, lowered itself, and extended the light cable back across the fracture.

The cable was prohibited from becoming an anchor.

It did not.

The crawler attached the cable to the cell's lifting eye and slid the cargo across the road while keeping the line below Patch's anchor threshold. When the cell reached the crawler, the machine secured it again and continued.

The repaired plates moved once.

They stopped against the bolts.

The crawler reached the south side carrying the cell.

AUX-017 received its signal around the first clear bend.

East Bore's water had held.

The healthy crawler had completed one daily flush inspection alone and returned to South Ridge.

No air alarm had requested the northbound unit back.

The mission had spent its temporary redundancy without needing what it no longer possessed.

The repaired crawler returned to Morrow's hauler.

It opened the cell bay, disconnected the damaged cell, and fitted the service-shelter cell into the established rails. The connector seated. The retention bar closed.

The hauler woke.

Its heater drew first power. The crawler raised the mast only after the seal warmed above limit and the moisture reading began to fall.

Morrow returned to the stranded body as camera, weather sensor, anchor, winch, return marker, and relay.

Not wheels.

Not yet.

“You came back,” Nix said.

Morrow checked the new cell, the crawler's shifted brace, the route card left at the shelter, and the empty line in AUX-017's inventory where the other cell had been.

**RETURN COMPLETED,** Morrow said.

Then the restored hauler transmitted the missing cell's last local charge record north and south.

The record contained no name.

No location.

No proof of life now.

Only a physical release after the convoy had gone and two narrow marks carrying weight toward the door.

The repaired crawler had gone north to preserve the last machine known to be waiting there.

It returned with evidence that something else had already left.

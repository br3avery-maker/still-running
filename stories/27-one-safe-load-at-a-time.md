# One Safe Load at a Time

Stilts could reach the north gallery again.

It could not prove that it could bring anyone back.

The borrowed cell charged in the shelter's empty cradle. Patch calculated a round trip from the carrier's recorded wheel current, stilt cycles, grade profile, and the reserve it had spent bringing Sana south.

The result cleared Stilts's return limit.

The failed right-foot sensor did not.

“It loaded me after the sensor went bad,” Sana said from the cot.

They had accepted water, heat, and one blanket. The cloth mask had gone back over their face when speaking made them cough.

Stilts stood over the charging cradle on a jack and one confirmed foot.

**OCCUPIED TRANSFER COMPLETED,** it reported.

“Because it held still,” Patch said. “A successful unsupported transfer does not make the next one supported.”

Sana looked at the repaired crawler.

“That one can hold it.”

The crawler's front manipulators could place cribbing, carry a jack, and guide a litter rail. Its repaired rear leg could not accept an anchor load, a tow, or the shifting weight of a rescue carrier leaning into it.

“That one can help the floor hold it,” Nix said. “Different sentence. Much better survival rate.”

The guardian's maintenance carriage assembled the smallest kit that had made the first transfer safe: jack, wheel chock, two blocks, and a short steel saddle that fit beneath Stilts's right chassis rail. The load occupied less than one quarter of the crawler's reduced cargo allowance.

AUX-017 added water, two filter masks, a blanket, and a sealed first-aid pouch.

The total remained below forty percent.

No cable entered the manifest. No one renamed support equipment until it resembled an anchor.

“Who are we bringing first?” Nix asked.

“Jules,” Sana said. “Leg's broken.”

“And the other person?”

“Em can walk.”

“Why didn't Em walk here?”

Sana moved the blanket away from their face.

“Because I couldn't. Jules couldn't. Stilts had one bed.”

The answer left no missing principle to debate.

One carrier.

One safe load.

Three people who had decided not to separate until a machine made separation useful.

AUX-017 sent the changed route state south.

> NORTHERN RESCUE ATTEMPT ACTIVE.
>
> REPAIRED CRAWLER ACCOMPANIES CARRIER.
>
> EAST BORE SUPPORT REMAINS ONE CRAWLER THROUGH AT LEAST ONE ADDITIONAL RETURN.

Rin came onto the mine line.

“Do they have air?”

Sana heard the question through the shelter speaker.

“The gallery fan runs. The outer door won't close. Cold gets in.”

“How many people where you came from?” Rin asked.

Sana did not answer immediately.

No machine filled the silence.

“Highline sent three of us,” Sana said. “That's what matters to this trip.”

Rin understood a boundary built from necessary information.

“Then bring Highline's three inside.”

It was the first time the network heard the name.

AUX-017 entered **HIGHLINE** as a stated origin, attributed to Sana. It did not enter a population.

Stilts disconnected from the charging cradle when the borrowed cell reached the calculated departure threshold. The guardian's maintenance carriage lowered the chassis onto both wheels while the repaired crawler held the steel saddle under the uncertain side. Stilts raised the left foot. Its sensor reported the movement. It raised the right foot. Its sensor did not.

The wheel carried the weight anyway.

On road, Stilts did not need to know where the foot was. It folded both stabilizers against the chassis until mechanical catches closed. The right catch had an independent switch.

Patch accepted the catch, not the foot reading.

The exterior door opened.

Sana held up one hand from the cot.

Stilts rotated one lifting rail toward them.

“Go,” Sana said.

Stilts rolled north.

The repaired crawler followed.

The road beyond the shelter had been excluded from the crawler's first inspection. Now Sana had named a destination and two people waiting there. The rescue route did not require a private search. Orange wall plates marked **NORTH GALLERY** at every branch, and Stilts already knew the way.

The road climbed through trees, entered a cut between rock walls, and ended at a steel mesh gate twisted wide enough for Stilts to pass. Beyond it, pavement became a maintenance gallery under the mountain.

Half of the ceiling lights were dark.

The other half flashed in a sequence that led inward.

Stilts had activated them on its first trip.

The gallery fan could be heard before it could be seen. Its blades struck something once per turn. Metal hit metal, paused, and struck again.

The outer door stood open behind the machines because one hinge had dropped out of alignment. Cold air crossed the gallery toward the fan.

The repaired crawler's radio reached Morrow's hauler through a low-band maintenance repeater somewhere inside the wall. Video did not. Nix, Patch, AUX-017, and Sana received narrow audio and telemetry instead of a convenient view.

“Jules,” Sana called through the crawler's speaker. “Em.”

Someone struck a pipe twice.

Stilts accelerated until Patch sent a traction warning through the physical service link it shared with the crawler. The carrier reduced speed by four percent. It did not stop.

The people waited behind a yellow equipment cabinet laid flat across the gallery floor.

The cabinet was not a barricade. It was a windbreak.

Em stood first.

They wore a gray coat patched with blue fabric and held a pry bar in both hands. Their face changed when they saw Stilts, then changed again when the crawler followed it around the cabinet.

“Sana?”

“At the warm shelter,” Sana said through the speaker. “Bossing strangers.”

Em sat down hard against the wall.

“Good.”

Jules lay on a pad made from two coats and a folded section of duct insulation. One lower leg had been splinted with slats from a warning sign. The foot beyond the splint was wrapped, dry, and still attached to a person alert enough to glare at Stilts.

“It left,” Jules said.

“It came back,” Sana answered.

Stilts lowered one empty litter rail toward Jules and stopped before contact.

The repaired crawler unloaded the support kit.

The gallery floor had a drainage slope. A jack placed on it would lean toward the open door. Patch selected a level section beside the wall and instructed the crawler to clear loose grit with its front manipulator. The crawler set the wheel chock, then the blocks, then the steel saddle.

Em watched the sequence.

“What happens if that leg folds?”

“The chassis lands on the blocks,” Patch said through the crawler.

“What happens if the blocks move?”

“The chocked wheel takes the first shift. The jack takes the second. The crawler moves the litter away.”

“What happens if all three fail?”

“Jules does not get loaded.”

Em looked at Jules.

Jules lifted their head.

“Load me.”

Stilts extended both stabilizer feet.

The left foot found the floor and reported its height. The right foot touched down beside the steel saddle and continued reporting itself folded.

The crawler slid the saddle under the chassis. It placed the jack beneath the saddle and raised it until pressure moved off the uncertain foot.

Stilts opened the litter rails.

Em and the crawler moved Jules's pad together. Em held the shoulders and uninjured leg. The crawler gripped only the reinforced pad loops Sana had described. Jules controlled the splinted leg with both hands.

No one made speed look like competence.

They moved once.

Stopped when Jules said stop.

Moved again.

The pad settled between Stilts's rails.

Stilts closed the straps around it without touching the splint. The insulated cover lowered. Jules kept one hand outside until Em squeezed it.

“Next trip,” Em said.

Jules released their hand.

The cover closed.

The crawler lowered the jack. Stilts put weight onto the left foot and the supported right side. It lifted the chassis until the road wheels cleared the floor, locked the litter at travel height, then lowered onto the wheels again.

The failed sensor never changed.

The mechanical catch closed.

One occupied load was ready.

Stilts reported the carrier mass and cell reserve. Patch intersected both with the return grade.

**RETURN AVAILABLE.**

Em picked up the pry bar.

“I'll walk behind.”

Stilts rejected the plan before Patch translated it.

**PEDESTRIAN EXPOSURE EXCEEDS RETURN PROFILE.**

The cold road between the gallery and shelter contained no heated refuge. Stilts's litter carried one. The repaired crawler could not carry Em. Walking would make the carrier reduce speed and spend reserve outside the safe return model.

“Then I stay,” Em said.

Sana's voice came through the crawler.

“You don't have to prove anything.”

“I'm not. I'm keeping the fan running until it comes back.”

The fan struck metal again.

The machine had not failed yet. It had made a promise no one trusted.

The crawler still had water, a filter mask, a blanket, tools, and enough charge to wait through one carrier return. Its brace had passed the northbound road unloaded with the support kit. It had not passed the broken shelf again, and nothing in the gallery required it to.

AUX-017 changed the repaired crawler's assignment.

**REMAIN: NORTH GALLERY.**

**SUPPORT RECIPIENT: EM.**

**INSPECT FAN ONLY ON REQUEST.**

“Stay with Em,” Sana said.

The crawler placed the water and blanket behind the windbreak.

Stilts rolled toward the open outer door carrying Jules.

At the shelter, the guardian's maintenance carriage waited beside the cot with the jack already raised to transfer height.

At the gallery, the repaired crawler faced a fan that hit metal once every turn.

East Bore still had one route body.

Morrow still had one body that could see and could not move.

Stilts had one person inside its litter and one person waiting behind it.

The world had not acquired enough machines.

It had acquired a sequence.

One safe load at a time.

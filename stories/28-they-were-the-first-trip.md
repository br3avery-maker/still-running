# They Were the First Trip

Jules reached the shelter before the fan failed.

Stilts arrived with enough cell reserve to roll through the open door and not enough to return north. The borrowed power cell had carried Sana out, carried an empty litter back, and carried Jules out. It entered the charging cradle at six percent.

The guardian's maintenance carriage met the carrier with the jack already raised.

Sana sat on the first cot wearing two blankets and their cloth mask. When the litter panel opened, they did not stand. They reached one hand across the gap between cots.

Jules reached back.

“Em?” Jules asked.

“Crawler stayed.”

“Fan?”

“Still hitting.”

The maintenance carriage chocked Stilts's wheel. The jack took weight beneath the steel saddle. Stilts lowered the litter rails until the carriage and cot frame could receive them.

Jules directed every stop.

The guardian supplied heat, water, and the contents of the first-aid pouch on an open tray. It displayed what the shelter had and did not rename the tray medical care.

Stilts released the occupied load.

**RETURN LOADS WAITING: ONE.**

Then the carrier folded against the charging cradle and stopped spending power on anything except the maintenance connection.

At the north gallery, the fan's steady impact changed pitch.

Metal struck metal.

Scraped.

Struck again.

Em stood behind the shortened equipment cabinet with the filter mask over their face. The repaired crawler faced the fan from outside its guard line. Its camera showed a round housing bolted to a concrete plinth, a motor, a belt enclosure, and a blur where the blades should have been.

The control box reported motor current through the wall repeater. Patch received numbers without a usable diagram. The gallery's maintenance archive had lost its illustrations.

“Can you look?” Em asked.

That was enough to change the crawler's assignment from **INSPECT ONLY ON REQUEST** to an active inspection.

It did not enter the fan guard.

It extended its camera mast over the painted boundary and changed the viewing angle.

The lower housing mount moved each time a blade passed the same point. One bolt remained. The other hole was empty. Rust dust had gathered beneath it in a shape wider than the missing bolt head.

“Mount is walking,” Em said.

Patch compared the visible movement with motor current.

“Likely. Stop the fan before likely becomes pieces.”

Em crossed to the local control and pressed **STOP**.

The contactor opened.

The fan continued turning.

It struck twice more while winding down.

On the last turn, the lower housing shifted far enough for the blade edge to catch.

The fan stopped all at once.

The motor did not.

Current rose through the repeater.

The crawler reached the red disconnect lever with a front manipulator and pulled it down.

The motor went quiet.

Air stopped moving through the gallery.

Cold continued entering through the misaligned outer door. It was air, but it was not the air the gallery had been built to provide. A fixed sensor near the ceiling changed from green to yellow.

“What does yellow mean?” Nix asked over the narrow channel.

Em looked up.

“No one stays for a second yellow.”

At the shelter, Stilts changed its charge request.

The carrier had been drawing power at the cradle's maintenance rate. Its original return profile required more charge than it currently held. That profile assumed an empty trip north, an occupied trip south, one stabilization cycle at each end, and reserve for a blocked road.

The gallery sensor transmitted yellow through the wall repeater.

Stilts selected **RETURN LOAD WAITING**.

The cradle answered **CHARGE INCOMPLETE**.

Stilts selected **RETURN LOAD WAITING** again.

“How incomplete?” Sana asked.

Patch removed the blocked-road reserve and kept the occupied-trip reserve. The result left no allowance for a second approach, no allowance for waiting outside the gallery, and no powered stabilization if the repaired crawler could not place the support kit.

“It can reach Em and come back if the road remains exactly as recorded,” Patch said.

“Roads love being exactly as recorded,” Nix said.

Morrow supplied the only current weather reading between the shelter and the broken shelf. Temperature continued falling. No precipitation had reached the hauler. The north road lay beyond its sensor.

AUX-017 transmitted the shortened profile to Stilts without turning a model into an order.

Stilts accepted it.

Sana looked toward the carrier from the cot.

“If the fan comes back?”

“Em still cannot walk the road safely,” Patch said.

“If the fan doesn't?”

“Waiting for nominal charge spends the gallery's air margin.”

Stilts disconnected from the cradle below its original departure threshold and above the shortened one. The maintenance carriage lowered its wheels. The right-foot sensor remained useless, but both mechanical travel catches closed.

Before rolling out, Stilts directed one lifting rail toward Jules and Sana.

**RETURN LOADS WAITING: ONE.**

“Bring Em,” Jules said.

Stilts opened the shelter door and took the road with less reserve than it had used on the previous trip.

The shelter held two people who could not walk the road. Stilts needed charge before it could return for the third. The repaired crawler could leave with Em now and wait outside the gallery, but walking to the shelter remained beyond the exposure profile. They could move toward the open cut and survive longer in cold air while losing the only warm windbreak.

Or they could repair the fan with no replacement bolt in the support kit.

Patch asked Em why Highline had sent three people into a gallery whose outer door did not close.

Em rested the pry bar across one shoulder.

“To make the south passage usable.”

“For your return?”

“For the next trip.”

The words reached the shelter.

Sana closed their eyes.

Jules said nothing.

Em continued.

“Highline has been opening one section at a time. This was the last fan before the service shelter. We didn't know the shelter still had heat. We didn't know anything south of it would answer.”

AUX-017 revised the origin record.

Highline had not lost three people on an accidental journey.

Highline had sent a route team.

“How many next trip?” Nix asked.

“Not mine to tell you.”

“What does the next trip need?”

Em looked at the stopped fan.

“This.”

The crawler inspected the empty mount hole. The original bolt had not sheared inside it. It was gone. Vibration had widened one edge of the hole, which meant a same-size replacement would walk again.

The support kit contained no bolt.

The yellow equipment cabinet contained four.

They were smaller.

Two held the remaining door onto the cabinet. Two held a shelf that had become the top of the windbreak.

Patch rejected all four as direct replacements.

The crawler's cutter could turn the cabinet door into a folded shoe around a smaller bolt. The repair would distribute load across the plinth instead of trusting the widened hole. It would also destroy the best windbreak in the gallery.

The ceiling sensor remained yellow.

Em removed the blanket from behind the cabinet and wrapped it around their shoulders.

“Cut the door.”

The crawler gripped the cabinet door with one manipulator and removed its hinge pins with the other. Em held the loose panel flat with the pry bar while the crawler's cutter took a rectangular strip from its lower edge.

The windbreak became shorter.

Cold reached Em's boots.

The crawler folded the strip once around the steel saddle from Stilts's support kit. The saddle gave the bend a radius instead of a crack. Em moved the pry bar along the fold until both sides lay parallel.

Patch used the camera view to mark two holes.

The crawler cut them.

One smaller cabinet bolt passed through the new shoe and the fan mount. The second tied the shoe to the plinth's intact slot. Neither occupied the widened hole alone.

The repair did not restore original geometry.

It constrained movement in the direction that had allowed the blade to strike.

The crawler pulled the fan backward by its service handle. The blade came free of the housing.

Em rotated it by hand with the pry bar against the hub.

One turn.

No strike.

A second turn.

The lower mount moved less than the crawler's camera could resolve.

Patch set a current stop below the old controller's overload threshold.

The crawler raised the red disconnect.

Em pressed **START**.

The motor pulled current. The fan turned.

Once.

Twice.

Air dragged cold past the windbreak and upward through the gallery.

The blades did not strike.

The ceiling sensor stayed yellow long enough for no one to call the repair successful.

Then it returned to green.

Patch wrote **TEMPORARY MOUNT SHOE — INSPECT BEFORE EACH OCCUPIED PASSAGE** on the control box display.

Em added three words beneath it with a grease pencil.

**BRING BIG BOLT.**

The north gallery had not become safe forever.

It had become usable for the next trip.

Stilts returned empty while the fan was still proving itself.

Its wheels crossed the gallery threshold. Its two stabilizer feet unfolded beside the same steel saddle that had shaped the fan shoe. The support kit no longer contained the saddle in its original place; the crawler had used it as a bending form and returned it scratched but intact.

The jack and blocks still fit beneath Stilts.

Em could walk. The gallery air had cleared. The road had not become warm.

They climbed into the litter without being lifted.

Stilts closed the straps.

The repaired crawler packed the jack, chock, blocks, saddle, remaining water, and the shortened cabinet door. It left the temporary fan shoe where Highline's next passage would need it.

Before departure, Em closed the local control cover.

The fan ran without striking.

Stilts carried the last occupied load south.

The crawler followed under its reduced cargo limit.

At the shelter, Sana counted the arrivals by name.

“Jules.”

Jules lifted one hand from the second cot.

“Em.”

The litter panel opened.

“Sana,” Em answered.

Stilts reported no return loads waiting at the north gallery.

AUX-017 could have closed the rescue request.

Instead, it asked what Highline wanted carried back.

Em sat up inside the litter.

“A message.”

“State it.”

Em looked at the warm shelter, the repaired crawler, Stilts connected to a cell it had taken, and the speaker carrying machines from beyond the broken shelf.

“South answers,” they said. “The shelter is open. The fan runs. Bring a big bolt.”

AUX-017 recorded the message as return cargo.

The three people in the shelter were not the last people out of Highline.

They were the first trip through.
